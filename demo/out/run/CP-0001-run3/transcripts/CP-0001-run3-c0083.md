1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the source can show and what it cannot.

Here's the method on a parallel example, not your letter. Imagine a mill owner writes to his investors about a walkout: *"The loyal hands stayed at their looms, while agitators from outside the town pulled the rest away."*

- **Position and interest:** He's an owner reassuring investors, so he wants the walkout to look small and externally caused.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see who showed up at the mill gate. He couldn't see what workers said to each other at night, or why they left.
- **Word choice:** "Loyal" and "agitators" frame the sides before any evidence is given. "Walked out" or "organizers" would read differently.
- **Can show:** how owners described strikes, what he feared investors would think, and which workers he noticed. **Cannot show:** the workers' motives, or how many really wanted to stay.

That's the two-column thinking the unit describes, written out in prose.
