2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point, and the Reader's unit 3 method is built for it. The idea is that you aren't summarizing the letter. You're asking who made it, for whom, and what that means for what it can tell you. The steps:

1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the source can show and what it can't.

Here's a quick practice example on a made-up source, not your letter. Imagine a mill owner writes to his investors: *"A few outside agitators have misled our loyal hands. The mill stands ready to resume."*

- **Position and interest:** The owner wants investors to stay confident, so he has a reason to make the strike look small and caused by outsiders.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the mill's output and who showed up. He probably couldn't see what workers said among themselves or why they joined in.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" and "misled" imply the workers had no grievances of their own. "Spokesmen" would suggest the opposite.
- **Can show:** The owner's view, the language of authority, what he wanted investors to believe. **Can't show:** the workers' real motives.
