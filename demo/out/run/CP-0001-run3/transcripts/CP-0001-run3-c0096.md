2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck, and the worksheet is less mysterious once you see the pattern. Reader unit 3 gives a method, which is basically a two-column list written as prose:

1. **Position and interest:** Who made this, for whom, and what did they want the reader to believe or do?
2. **What they could see vs. what was out of view** from where they stood.
3. **Word choices:** places where the author picked one word when others were available.
4. **Can show / cannot show:** what the document reveals (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, whose names were recorded) versus what it can't (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness).

Here's a quick practice example on a made-up source, not yours. Imagine a mill owner writes to a newspaper: *"Agitators from outside misled our loyal hands into walking out."*
- **Position:** the owner wants the public to see the strike as the work of outsiders.
- **Word choices:** "agitators" and "misled" instead of "organizers" and "persuaded," which treats the workers as passive.
- **Can show:** how an employer wanted the strike understood, and what language he thought would win sympathy.
- **Cannot show:** why the workers actually left, since they never speak.
