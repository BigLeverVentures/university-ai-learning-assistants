1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who is this person, who are they writing to, and what do they want?
2. **What could they see, and what was out of view?** Think about where they stood and who they talked to.
3. **Notice word choices** where other words were available.
4. **State what the document can show and what it can't.** It can show the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, and the names that were recorded. It can't show the motives of the people described, or what happened where the author wasn't.

Your 150 words are basically those steps written as prose.

Here's a worked example on a different, invented source, so you can see the moves.

*Source: a mill owner's letter to the town council, describing a walkout at his mill.*

- **Perspective and interest:** The owner is writing to officials whose help he wants, so he has a reason to present the walkout as disorder and himself as the injured party.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the mill gate and the machinery. He couldn't see the workers' private meetings or their reasons.
- **Word choice:** He writes "mob" where "crowd" was available, which signals how he wants the council to react.
- **Can show:** How an employer framed a walkout to authorities, and which events he thought worth reporting.
- **Can't show:** Why the workers left, or whether "mob" describes what actually happened.
