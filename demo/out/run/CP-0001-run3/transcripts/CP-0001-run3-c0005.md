**The steps from Reader unit 3**
1. Name the author's position and interest. Who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

**A worked example on a different (invented) source**

Say a lighthouse keeper writes to the district inspector: *"The lamp was lit every night of the storm. My assistant, Voss, was idle and slept at his post. I alone kept the light."*

- **Position and interest:** The keeper is a supervisor reporting to a superior. He wants to look dependable and protect himself from blame.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the lamp and his own work. He couldn't see what Voss did while he himself was asleep or elsewhere, and Voss's side isn't there at all.
- **Word choice:** "Idle" and "slept" are judgments. "Resting between watches" was another possible description.
- **Can show:** How a keeper presented himself to authority, what he thought counted as good service, and that the lamp's operation mattered enough to report.
- **Can't show:** Whether Voss was actually negligent, or what really happened on each night of the storm.

Notice that the "can't show" side isn't just "he might be biased." It names specific things that were out of the author's view or that the document doesn't record.
