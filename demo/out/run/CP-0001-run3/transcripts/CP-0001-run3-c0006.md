2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point, and it usually means the task is less about summarizing the letter than about asking different questions of it. Reader unit 3 gives a method in four moves:

1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. **Sort what they could see from what was out of view.**
3. **Notice word choices** where other words were available.
4. **State what the source can show and what it can't.**

Here's a worked example on a made-up source, not yours.

*Source: a lighthouse keeper's report to the district inspector after a storm. He says he kept the lamp burning all night and that the cracked lens was the assistant keeper's carelessness.*

> The keeper wrote to protect his job, so the report shows what he wanted the inspector to believe. He could see the lamp and the tower, but not what the assistant did when they were apart. His word "carelessness" assigns blame instead of describing an event. The report can show how a keeper framed responsibility to a superior. It can't show what actually cracked the lens or what the assistant would say.

Notice that each sentence answers one of the four moves, and that the "can't show" claims point to things *outside the author's view or motives*.
