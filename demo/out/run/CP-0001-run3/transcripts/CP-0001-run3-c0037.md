**The method (unit 3):** evaluating a source means writing a two-column list in prose, "what it can show" and "what it cannot."
1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. Note what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

**Parallel example (not your source):** Imagine a short report from a canal lockkeeper to the canal company after boatmen refused to work, saying: "The boatmen have been misled by agitators. I stayed at my post throughout. Most are loyal and want to return."

1. **Position and interest:** The lockkeeper is a company employee writing to his employer. He wants to look loyal and competent.
2. **Could see / out of view:** He could see who gathered at the lock and what he was told. He could not see private meetings elsewhere or what the boatmen said among themselves.
3. **Word choices:** "Misled" and "agitators" suggest the boatmen couldn't think for themselves, where "organizers" or "spokesmen" were available. "Loyal" is his claim about people's feelings, which he can't observe directly.
4. **Can show / cannot show:** It can show how a company employee described a walkout, what vocabulary of authority he used, and which events he reported seeing. It cannot show why the boatmen acted or how many really wanted to return.
