1. **Position and interest:** who made the document, for whom, and what they wanted the reader to believe or do.
2. **What they could and couldn't see** from where they stood.
3. **Word choices** where other words were available.
4. **What the source can show** (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and **what it cannot show** (other people's motives, events outside the author's view).

Here's the method on a parallel example, not your letter. Imagine a mill owner writes to the town council: "A mob of agitators shut down my mill, though most of my loyal hands wanted to work."

- **Position and interest:** he's an owner asking the council for help, so he wants to look like the victim of outsiders.
- **Word choices:** he says "mob" and "agitators" where "crowd" or "organizers" were available.
- **Can show:** how owners described strikers, and what he hoped officials would accept.
- **Cannot show:** why the workers actually walked out, or whether "most wanted to work" is true. He can't see into their minds, and he has a reason to say it.
