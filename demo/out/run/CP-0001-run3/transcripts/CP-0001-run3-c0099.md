2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck. The worksheet doesn't ask you to summarize the letter. It asks you to treat it as evidence, and Reader unit 3 gives a sequence for that:

1. **Position and interest:** Who made it, for whom, and what did they want the reader to believe or do?
2. **Vantage point:** What could the author see from where they stood, and what was out of view?
3. **Word choices:** Where did they pick one word when others were available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What does the document reveal (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, whose names were recorded), and what can't it tell you (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness)?

Here is a **practice example on an invented source, not your assignment**. Imagine a mill owner's notice to the town paper: "A few agitators have misled our loyal workers into abandoning their posts."

- **Position:** The owner is writing for the public, and he wants to protect his reputation and pressure the strikers.
- **Vantage:** He can see the closed mill and the payroll, but not what was said in workers' meetings or why individuals joined.
- **Word choices:** "Agitators" rather than "organizers," "misled" rather than "persuaded," "abandoning" rather than "leaving."
- **Can show:** How an employer framed a strike for the public. **Cannot show:** Why the workers actually walked out.
