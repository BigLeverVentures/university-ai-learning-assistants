2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck, and the Reader's unit 3 gives you a way in. The task is a two-column list written as prose:

1. **Position and interest:** who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want that reader to believe or do?
2. **What the author could see and couldn't:** what was in view from where they stood, and what wasn't?
3. **Word choices:** places where the author picked one word over another available one.
4. **Can show / cannot show:** what the document reveals (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, whose names were recorded) versus what it can't (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness).

Here's a short practice example on a different, made-up source, not your letter.

*Practice source: a mill owner writes to the town newspaper, saying "a handful of agitators drove loyal hands from their looms."*
- **Position:** the owner, writing to the public, wants to look reasonable and protect his reputation.
- **Could see:** the empty mill and the crowd outside. **Couldn't see:** why individual workers walked out, or what was said at their meetings.
- **Word choice:** "agitators" instead of "organizers," and "driven" instead of "chose to leave."
- **Can show:** how an owner wanted the strike to look, and what language he used for workers. **Can't show:** whether workers were coerced or acted on their own grievances.

Notice that every claim in that example points to specific words or to the author's position. That's the evidence versus inference habit your worksheet needs. When you make a claim, tie it to words in the source and be clear about which parts are your inference.
