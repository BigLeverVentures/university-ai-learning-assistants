2. **assistant**: That's a common spot to be in, and it usually means the task is less "summarize the letter" and more "ask who made it and what that does to what it can tell you." Reader unit 3 lays out the steps:

1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the source can show and what it cannot.

Here's a quick practice example on an invented source, not your packet. Imagine a mill owner's letter to his shareholders about a walkout:

> *"The weavers were led astray by agitators, and production stopped. I have kept the looms oiled and ready."*

- **Position and interest:** He's an owner reporting to investors, so he wants to look in control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the mill floor and the stopped looms. He probably couldn't see what was said at workers' meetings or what their households were facing.
- **Word choice:** "Led astray" implies the workers had no reasons of their own. "Agitators" could have been "organizers."
- **Can show:** how an owner framed a strike for investors, and what he chose to emphasize. **Cannot show:** why the weavers actually walked out.

Notice that this analysis starts from who wrote the source and why, and only then turns to what it says.
