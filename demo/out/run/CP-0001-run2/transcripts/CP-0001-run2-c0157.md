**Practice source (made up for this exercise):** A mill owner's statement printed in a town newspaper, March 1890: *"Our weavers were content until outside agitators filled their heads with notions. The mill has always paid fair wages. A few malcontents have now locked the gates against honest workers who wish to return."*

**Worked analysis, starting with provenance:**

- **Who, for whom, when?** The mill owner wrote for the town's newspaper readers, during the strike, so he was trying to win public sympathy.
- **What he could see:** the mill's finances and his own wage decisions, plus whatever his managers told him.
- **What was out of view:** what weavers said to one another, and what their wages bought.
- **Word choices:** "agitators" instead of "organizers," "malcontents" instead of "strikers," "honest workers" instead of "those who crossed the line." Each choice frames the strikers as troublemakers.

**In prose:** The statement shows how an employer wanted the public to see the strike: as outside interference with satisfied workers. It shows the vocabulary of authority. It cannot show whether wages were fair, why the weavers walked out, or how many wanted to return. Those are claims, not evidence.

Notice that the analysis moves from author and purpose, to vantage point, to word choice, to the limits of what the source can show.
