2. **assistant**: That's a common spot to get stuck. The worksheet isn't asking what the letter says. It's asking you to treat the letter as something a particular person made, for a particular reader, and then say what it can and can't tell us. Reader unit 3 breaks this into steps:

1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices, where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

Here's a **practice example on made-up material**, not your assigned source:

> *A mill owner writes to his company's board: "Agitators from outside drew off our hands at the gate. The loyal workers were frightened into leaving."*
>
> - **Position and interest:** An owner reporting upward, who wants the board to see him as in control and not at fault.
> - **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the gate and who left. He couldn't hear the workers' own conversations or know their reasons.
> - **Word choice:** "Agitators" rather than "organizers." "Frightened" rather than "persuaded."
> - **Can show:** How an owner explained a strike to his board, and the vocabulary of authority. **Cannot show:** Why the workers actually left, or whether they were frightened.

Notice that the last step separates what the source *shows* from what we'd only be *inferring*.
