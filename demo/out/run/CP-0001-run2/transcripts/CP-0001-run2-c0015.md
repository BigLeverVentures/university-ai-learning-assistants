2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck, and the worksheet is asking for less than it seems. Unit 3 of the Reader gives you a sequence, and your 150 words are just that sequence written as prose:

1. **Author's position and interest:** Who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. **What they could see and what was out of view:** What was this person in a position to know firsthand? What happened elsewhere or inside other people's heads?
3. **Word choices:** Where did they pick one word when others were available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What does the document reveal (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded), and what can't it tell you (others' motives, events the author didn't witness)?

Here is a worked example on a parallel source I made up, not your letter:

*A harbor master writes to the town council after a fishermen's boycott of the docks: "The boats sat idle because a few agitators frightened honest crews. I counted the empty moorings each morning."*

- **Position/interest:** He answers to the council and wants to look like he kept control.
- **Seen/not seen:** He could count moorings, but he wasn't in the fishermen's meetings, so he can't know why they stayed ashore.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" and "frightened" rather than "organizers" and "persuaded."
- **Shows/can't show:** It shows what an official wanted the council to believe and how he labeled people. It can't show the crews' real reasons.

Notice that the example never retells the letter. It says what kind of evidence the letter is.
