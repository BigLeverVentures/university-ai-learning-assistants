2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point, and it usually means the task is less about summarizing the letter than about asking what kind of document it is. Reader unit 3 gives a sequence:

1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. **List what they could see and what was out of view** from where they stood.
3. **Notice word choices** where other words were available.
4. **State what the source can show and what it cannot.** Unit 3 calls this a two-column list, written in prose.

Here is a worked example on a made-up source, so you can see the moves. Suppose a school principal writes to the school board: *"The students walked out of assembly at ten. I believe most were misled by a few troublemakers, and I kept the building secure."*

*Position:* The principal answers to the board and wants to look in control. *Could see:* the walkout itself, the building, who he disciplined. *Out of view:* what students said to each other, and why they left. *Word choice:* "troublemakers" instead of "organizers," and "misled" assumes the others had no reasons of their own. *Can show:* how an administrator described the event and what he thought the board wanted to hear. *Cannot show:* the students' motives, or whether "most" really opposed the walkout.

Notice that the claim about "most were misled" is treated as evidence of the author's view, not as a fact about the students. That's the "against the grain" move.
