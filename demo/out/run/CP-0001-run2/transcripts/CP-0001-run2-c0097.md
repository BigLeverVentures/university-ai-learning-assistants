**The method (Reader, unit 3):**
1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

Your 150 words are basically that two-column list (can show / cannot show) written as prose.

**Parallel example (invented, not your assignment):**
*A mill overseer writes to the owner: "The weavers walked out Tuesday. Most were led astray by two troublemakers, Doyle and Marsh. I stayed at my post and kept the looms oiled."*

1. **Position and interest:** He's a supervisor reporting upward. He wants the owner to see him as loyal and in control.
2. **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see who left and when, and he could see the mill floor. He couldn't see what the weavers said to each other or what happened in their meetings.
3. **Word choices:** "Led astray" assumes the weavers had no minds of their own. "Troublemakers" could have been "organizers."
4. **Can show:** The overseer's view, the language managers used about workers, and which names he recorded. **Cannot show:** why the weavers actually left, or what they thought of Doyle and Marsh.

Notice that the worked example did more than summarize the source. It asked what the author wanted and what the choice of words reveals.
