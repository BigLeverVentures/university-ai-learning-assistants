**The method (Reader unit 3)**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who wrote it, to whom, and what they wanted.
2. Note what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices, where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it cannot (others' motives, events the author didn't witness).

**Worked example (parallel material, not your source)**
Imagine a ship's captain writes to the owners: *"The crew refused duty on Tuesday at the instigation of the cook, who has long been troublesome. The men are sullen but would obey if properly led. I have restored order."*

- *Position and interest:* The captain reports to his employers and wants to appear in control, so he has a reason to blame one person and downplay the problem.
- *Seen and unseen:* He saw the refusal firsthand. He couldn't see what the crew said among themselves below deck.
- *Word choice:* "Instigation" and "troublesome" suggest the cook led the crew, where "spokesman" would suggest the crew had its own grievances.
- *Can show:* How a captain explained a dispute to owners, and who he chose to name. *Cannot show:* Why the crew actually refused, or whether the cook led anyone.

Notice that the last step says what the source is evidence *of*, which isn't the same as what happened.
