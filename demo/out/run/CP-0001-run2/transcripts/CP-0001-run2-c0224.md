**What the task asks (Reader unit 3):** You're not summarizing the letter. You're judging it as evidence, in three moves:
1. **Position and interest:** who wrote it, to whom, and what they wanted the reader to believe or do.
2. **What they could see vs. what was out of view** from where they stood.
3. **Two columns, written as prose:** what the document *can* show and what it *cannot*. The Reader also suggests noticing word choices where other words were available.

**Practice example (not your source):** Imagine a mill owner's notice to the town, saying that "agitators" caused a walkout and that "most loyal hands" want to return.
- *Position and interest:* the owner wants public sympathy and wants to protect his reputation.
- *Could see / couldn't see:* he could see the closed gates and his ledgers, but not what workers said among themselves.
- *Word choice:* "agitators" instead of "organizers" frames the leaders as outsiders.
- *Can show:* how owners described strikers, and which events they chose to report. *Cannot show:* why workers actually walked out, or how many wanted to return.

Notice that the claims are tied to specific words in the source, and the inferences ("he wants sympathy") are marked as inferences.
