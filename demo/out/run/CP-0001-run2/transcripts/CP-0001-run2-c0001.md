**The method (Reader unit 3):** Evaluating a source means building a two-column list, then writing it as prose:
1. **Position and interest:** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want?
2. **Vantage point:** What could the author see from where they stood, and what was out of view?
3. **Word choices:** Where did they pick one word when others were available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What does the document reveal (the author's view, the language of authority, the names recorded), and what can't it tell you (the motives of the people described, events the author didn't witness)?

Your 150 words answer three questions: whose perspective, what it can show, and what it cannot.

**Worked example on an invented source (not yours):** Imagine a harbor master's note to the town council saying, "The dockhands *refused* to unload the barge, and a *mob* gathered. I saw none of what was said inside the warehouse."

- *Perspective:* A town official reporting upward, likely wanting to show he handled things.
- *Can show:* How an official described dockhands, and that he thought "mob" would persuade the council.
- *Cannot show:* Why the dockhands refused, or what happened in the warehouse, since he admits he wasn't there.

In prose: "This note gives the harbor master's view, written to justify his handling of the dispute. It shows the vocabulary officials used for workers, but it cannot show the dockhands' reasons or what occurred indoors."
