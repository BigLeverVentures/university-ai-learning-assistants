**Practice source (made up):** A harbor master writes to the town council after dockworkers stopped unloading ships: *"Only a few troublemakers kept the men from their duty. The rest were willing. I sent the ringleader away and the quay is quiet."*

**Worked analysis, following the Reader's steps:**

- **Position and interest:** The harbor master answers to the council. He wants them to think he kept control.
- **What he could see:** He saw the quay and who stood where. He could not see what dockworkers said among themselves at home or in the taverns.
- **Word choice:** He wrote "troublemakers" and "ringleader" where "organizers" or "spokesman" were available. Those words cast the protest as the work of a few rather than a shared grievance.
- **Can show:** How an official described a stoppage, and what he assumed the council wanted to hear.
- **Cannot show:** Why the dockworkers stopped, or whether "the rest were willing" is true. That is his claim, not evidence.

Notice that the last point separates what the source says from what we can actually know. That's the evidence/inference distinction.
