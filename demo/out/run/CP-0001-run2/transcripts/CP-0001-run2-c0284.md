**What the worksheet asks for.** The Reader frames evaluating a source as a two-column list written in prose:
1. **Who made it, for whom, and what did they want?** Name the author's position and interest.
2. **What could the author see, and what was out of view?**
3. **Which words did they choose** when other words were available?
4. **What can the document show, and what can't it?** For example, the author's view and vocabulary versus other people's motives.

**Practice example (not your document).** Imagine a mill owner's note to his business partner after a walkout: *"The hands deserted their posts at noon, led astray by agitators. Most are loyal."*
- *Perspective and interest:* an owner reporting to a partner, who wants to look in control.
- *Word choice:* "deserted" and "agitators" frame leaving as a betrayal, where "walked out" and "organizers" would not.
- *Can show:* how owners talked about workers, and what he wanted his partner to believe.
- *Can't show:* why the workers actually left, or whether "most are loyal" is true, since he has no way of knowing and every reason to say it.

Notice that I never judged whether the owner was right. I asked what the note reveals and where its view runs out.
