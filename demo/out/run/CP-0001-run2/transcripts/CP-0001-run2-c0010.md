**The steps**
1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who is writing, to whom, and what do they want from the reader?
2. **What could the author see, and what was out of view?** Think about where they were standing and who they knew.
3. **Notice word choices.** Look for places where the author picked one word when another was available.
4. **Two columns, in prose.** What can the document show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded)? What can't it show (the motives of the people described, events where the author wasn't)?

**A worked example on parallel material (not your source)**

Imagine a short invented note from a ship's mate to the captain: *"The crew refused the evening watch. Two troublemakers, Ames and Doyle, stirred up the rest. I believe most would obey if left alone."*

- **Position and interest:** The mate is a subordinate reporting upward, so he wants to look in control and not to blame.
- **Seen and not seen:** He saw the refusal itself, but not what the crew said among themselves.
- **Word choice:** "Troublemakers" instead of "objectors," and "stirred up" suggests the crew had no reasons of their own.
- **Shows and can't show:** It shows how an officer talked about dissent and which crew members he named. It can't show why the crew refused, or whether Ames and Doyle really led anything.
