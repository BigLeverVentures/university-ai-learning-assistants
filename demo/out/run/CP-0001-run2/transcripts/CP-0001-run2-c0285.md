**The method (Reader, unit 3)**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who wrote it, to whom, and why.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it cannot (others' motives, events where the author wasn't present).

**Practice example (not your assignment)**
Imagine a mill owner's note to his lawyer after a walkout: *"The hands deserted their looms at noon, led by agitators from outside. The loyal majority is cowed."*

- **Position:** The owner is writing to his lawyer, so he has reason to present himself as the wronged party.
- **Could see:** the empty mill and the timing. **Couldn't see:** what was said among the workers.
- **Word choices:** "deserted" rather than "left," and "agitators" rather than "organizers." "Loyal majority" is a claim, not something he could count.
- **Can show:** how an owner framed a strike to a legal ally. **Cannot show:** why the workers actually walked out.

Notice that the last step separates what the source shows from what it only claims.
