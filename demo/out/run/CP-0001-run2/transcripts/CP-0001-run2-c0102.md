2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point. The worksheet asks for a two-column list written as prose, and the Reader unit 3 method breaks it into steps.

**The method**
1. **Author's position and interest:** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want from the reader?
2. **What they could see vs. what was out of view:** Where was the author standing, and what happened where they weren't?
3. **Word choices:** Where did the author pick one word when others were available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What does the document reveal (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded), and what can't it tell you (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness)?

**Worked example on a different, invented source**

Suppose a ship's captain writes to the shipowners in 1850: *"The crew refused duty on the 4th. The cook and two seamen were the instigators. I restored order by the 6th."*

- *Position and interest:* The captain is reporting to his employers, so he wants to look competent and in control.
- *Seen vs. unseen:* He saw the deck, but not what the crew said among themselves below.
- *Word choice:* "Instigators" and "restored order" assume the crew was at fault, where "complained" or "grievance" would not.
- *Can show:* How a captain explained a dispute to his bosses, and which crew members he named. *Cannot show:* why the crew refused duty, or whether the "instigators" were really leaders.

The prose version of that would combine those points into a few sentences, with a clear "this shows… but not…" structure.
