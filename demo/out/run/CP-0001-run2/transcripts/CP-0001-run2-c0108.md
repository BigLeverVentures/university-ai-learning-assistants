**Worked example on a parallel source (invented, not your assignment)**

Imagine a note from a mill overseer, Mr. Bell, to the mill owner after a walkout of spinners: *"The girls left their frames at noon, led astray by two outsiders. Most were loath to go. I have kept the machinery oiled."*

- **Position and interest:** Bell is a supervisor reporting upward. He has a stake in looking like he controlled the situation.
- **Could see:** the mill floor, the moment of the walkout. **Out of view:** what was said among the workers, and what happened after they left the building.
- **Word choices:** "led astray" suggests the workers had no reasons of their own. "Loath" is a claim about feelings he couldn't directly observe.
- **Can show:** how an overseer framed a walkout to an owner, and that he blamed outsiders. **Cannot show:** why the spinners actually left, or how many wanted to.

In prose, that might begin: *"Bell's note reveals less about the walkout than about what an overseer wanted his employer to believe..."*

**Why audience matters**
- **What gets included:** Writers put in what their reader needs or will approve of. A report to a boss is selective in a way a private diary or a letter to a friend wouldn't be.
