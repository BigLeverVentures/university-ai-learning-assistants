**The method (Reader unit 3)**

The worksheet asks you to evaluate a source as a two-column list written in prose:

1. **Position and interest:** Who made it, for whom, and what did they want the reader to believe or do?
2. **What they could see and what was out of view:** What was the author in a position to know firsthand, and what happened where they weren't?
3. **Word choices:** Where did the author pick one word when another was available? The Reader's example is "ringleaders" versus "spokesmen."
4. **Can show / can't show:** What does the source reveal, even unintentionally (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, whom they bothered to name)? What can it not tell you, such as the motives of the people it describes?

**A parallel example (not your source)**

Imagine a mill owner's letter to his investors about a walkout. He writes: "A handful of agitators pulled the hands off the floor, though most were loyal."

- *Position and interest:* He's answering to investors and wants to look in control.
- *Could and couldn't see:* He saw the empty floor, but he wasn't at the workers' meetings.
- *Word choice:* "Agitators" and "hands" instead of "organizers" and "workers."
- *Can show:* how owners talked about labor, and that he wants the walkout to look small. *Can't show:* why the workers actually left, or whether most were really "loyal."
