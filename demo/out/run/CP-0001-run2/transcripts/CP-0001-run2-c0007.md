2. **assistant**: That's a common spot to get stuck. The assignment isn't asking whether the letter is true or false. It's asking you to treat the letter as evidence and say what kind of evidence it is. Unit 3 of the Reader breaks that into four moves:

1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want?
2. **What could the author see, and what was out of view?**
3. **Notice word choices** where other words were available.
4. **State what the source can show and what it cannot.**

Here's a worked example on made-up material, not your letter. Imagine a lighthouse keeper writes to the harbor board after a ship runs aground: *"The captain ignored my signal and steered carelessly onto the rocks. My light was burning properly all night."*

- **Position and interest:** The keeper could be blamed for the wreck, so he wants the board to believe the light worked.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the ship's approach from shore, but not what happened on the bridge or what the captain was thinking.
- **Word choice:** "Carelessly" is a judgment, where "off course" would just describe.
- **Can show:** the keeper's version of events, how he defended himself, and what he thought the board would accept. **Cannot show:** why the captain steered as he did, or whether the light was really visible.

Notice that the last step separates *what the document reveals* from *what it can't know*. That's the core of your 150 words.
