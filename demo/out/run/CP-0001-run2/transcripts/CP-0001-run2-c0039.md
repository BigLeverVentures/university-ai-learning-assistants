**Practice source (invented):** A ferry operator writes to the town council after dockworkers refused to unload a cargo boat. "The crew downed tools at noon. Their spokesman, a deckhand named Ames, made unreasonable demands. I believe most of the men were misled. The boat is secure."

**Step 1: Position and interest.** The writer is the operator, writing to the council that oversees his license. He has a stake in looking competent and in need of no outside intervention.

**Step 2: Evidence, then inference.**
- *Evidence:* He calls the demands "unreasonable" but doesn't say what they were.
- *Inference:* He may not have wanted the council to weigh the demands for itself.
- *Evidence:* He names only Ames.
- *Inference:* Ames may have been the only worker he knew, or the only one he thought worth blaming.

**Step 3: Can and cannot show.**
- *Can show:* how an operator described a work stoppage to an authority, and which worker he singled out.
- *Cannot show:* what the crew wanted, why they acted, or whether "most of the men were misled" is true. He may not have been in a position to know.
