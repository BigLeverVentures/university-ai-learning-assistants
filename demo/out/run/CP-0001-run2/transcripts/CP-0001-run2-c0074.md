**The core idea:** every document was made by someone, for someone, to do something. So a source can't be treated as a window onto what happened. It's a window onto what *that person, in that position, wanted a particular reader to believe or do*. That's the perspective part.

**Two ways to read it:**
- **With the grain:** take the document on its own terms. What does it say, and what does it want its first reader to think or do?
- **Against the grain:** ask what it reveals *without meaning to*. The Reader's example is that a foreman's report tells you about a strike, but also what he thought his boss wanted to hear, which workers he knew by name, and which he didn't.

**Can show vs. cannot show:** a source can show the author's view, the language of his position, and the names he chose to record. It cannot show the inner motives of the people he describes, or events outside his view. You're sorting claims into "this tells us about the author" and "this would require someone else's evidence."
