2. **assistant**: That's a common spot to get stuck, and the assignment is less mysterious than it looks. Unit 3 of the Reader gives you a sequence, and the worksheet is that sequence written up in about 150 words:

1. **Author's position and interest:** who wrote it, to whom, and what they wanted.
2. **What they could see vs. what was out of view** from where they stood.
3. **Word choices** where other words were available.
4. **Can show / cannot show:** what the document reveals (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it can't (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness).

Here's the method on a parallel source, not your letter. Imagine an invented 1890 report from a lighthouse keeper to the harbor board about a night when a ship ran aground.

- **Position and interest:** The keeper is an employee reporting to his supervisors, and he wants to show he kept the light burning properly.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the lamp, the weather, and the ship's lights from the tower. He couldn't see what happened on deck or why the captain chose his course.
- **Word choices:** He writes that the ship "ignored" the warning light. "Ignored" assumes the crew saw it and chose not to, which he can't know. "Failed to respond" or "did not alter course" would claim less.
- **Can show / cannot show:** The report can show how the keeper wanted his duty to look, what the board expected of him, and what he recorded about the weather. It cannot show the captain's reasons or what happened aboard.

In a worksheet, those four points would become a few connected sentences rather than a list.
