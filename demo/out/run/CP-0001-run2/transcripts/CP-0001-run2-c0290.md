6. **assistant**: Yes, it matters a lot. It's the core idea in Reader unit 3: "Every document was made by someone, for someone, to do something." Audience shapes what an author includes, what they leave out, and which words they pick.

**Why it matters:**
- Authors write to be believed by their reader. A report to a boss is shaped by what the writer wants the boss to think of him, not just by what happened.
- Audience affects what counts as safe to say. Someone writing to a superior might stress his own diligence and put blame elsewhere. Someone writing to a friend might admit doubt or confusion.
- It sets the limits of what you can conclude. If the letter was written to impress one man, you can't treat it as a neutral account of the strike. You can still learn from it, but the question changes to "what does this show about how a foreman spoke to authority?"

**A parallel example (not your source):** Imagine a ship's captain writes two accounts of the same storm. One goes to the shipping company, the other to his sister. Which do you expect to mention the damage he might be blamed for? Which might admit he was afraid? The storm is the same, but the audience changes the story.
