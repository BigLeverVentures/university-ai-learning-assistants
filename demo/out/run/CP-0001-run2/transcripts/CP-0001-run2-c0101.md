2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point, so let me show you how the Reader's method works, using a made-up source rather than your letter.

**Parallel source (invented):** A lockkeeper writes to the canal company after a boatmen's work stoppage: *"The boatmen have been idle three days. They were stirred up by a pair of outsiders, and I believe most would work if the tolls were not raised. I have kept the locks open and the books in order."*

Reader unit 3 gives four moves:

1. **Author's position and interest.** He is a company employee reporting to his employer. He wants to look loyal and in control, so he mentions the open locks and orderly books.
2. **What he could see and what was out of view.** He could see the boatmen at the locks and who showed up. He could not see their private conversations or what they discussed away from his post.
3. **Word choices.** "Stirred up" and "outsiders" suggest the boatmen wouldn't have stopped on their own. "Idle" is a different word from "refused" or "withheld."
4. **Can show / cannot show.**
   - *Can show:* the company's view of the stoppage, the vocabulary of authority, and what he thought the owner wanted to hear.
   - *Cannot show:* why the boatmen actually stopped, or what happened beyond his view.

Your worksheet is that two-column list, **written as prose** in about 150 words. The prompt's three parts (perspective, can show, cannot show) map onto those four moves.
