**The idea in unit 3:** every document was made by someone, for someone, to do something. So you read it two ways:
- **With the grain:** what does it say, and what does it want its first reader to believe or do?
- **Against the grain:** what does it show without meaning to?

**The steps the Reader gives:**
1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it cannot (the motives of the people described, what happened where the author wasn't).

**A parallel example (not your source):** Imagine a landlord's letter to the city council saying, "The tenants were disorderly and refused to leave." The landlord's interest is getting the council on his side. He could see the building's front steps but not what tenants said among themselves. He chose "disorderly" where "angry" or "protesting" was available. So the letter can show how landlords described tenants and what he wanted the council to do. It can't show why the tenants stayed or what happened inside.
