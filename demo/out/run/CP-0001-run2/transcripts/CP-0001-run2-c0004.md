**The method (Reader unit 3), in short**
1. **Perspective and interest:** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want from that reader?
2. **Vantage point:** What could the author see from where they stood, and what was out of view?
3. **Word choices:** Where did they pick one word when another was available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What does the document reveal (even unintentionally), and what can't it tell you?

Your 150 words are basically that two-column list written as prose.

**Worked example (invented, not your source)**
*A lighthouse keeper writes to the district inspector after a ship ran aground: "The vessel ignored my signals. The crew were careless and the captain, I am told, had been drinking. The light was burning properly all night."*

- **Perspective/interest:** The keeper could be blamed for the wreck, so he wants the inspector to see the light as working and the fault as the crew's.
- **Vantage point:** He saw the ship's approach from the tower. He couldn't see the deck, so "had been drinking" is hearsay ("I am told").
- **Word choices:** "Ignored" and "careless" assign blame. "Did not respond" would have been neutral.
- **Can show:** How a keeper defended himself, and what an official might expect to hear. **Cannot show:** why the ship really ran aground, or what the crew experienced.

Notice that the "can show" side includes things the writer didn't mean to reveal.
