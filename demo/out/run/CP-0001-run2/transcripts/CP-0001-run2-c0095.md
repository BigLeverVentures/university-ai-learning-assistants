2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point, and the confusion usually comes from not knowing what "evaluate" means here. Reader unit 3 gives a method: you're not summarizing the letter, you're asking what it shows and what it can't.

**The steps (from Reader unit 3):**
1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

**A worked example on a different, made-up source.** Say a ship's captain writes to the owners: *"The crew refused duty on Tuesday. The mutineers are led by Doyle, a deckhand, and the cook. Most of the men are loyal and were intimidated. The cargo is secure."*

- **Position and interest:** The captain is writing to the owners and is responsible for the ship, so he wants to look in control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw who refused orders on deck. He probably didn't hear what the crew said among themselves below.
- **Word choice:** "Mutineers" instead of "men who refused duty." "Intimidated" instead of "disagreed."
- **Can show:** The captain's view, the language of authority, and which crew members he named. **Cannot show:** why the crew refused, or whether most really were "loyal."

Written as prose, that two-column list becomes your evaluation.
