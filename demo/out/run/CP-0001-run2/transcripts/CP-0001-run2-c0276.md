2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck, and it usually means the task is less "summarize the letter" than "say what kind of evidence it is." The Reader's unit 3 method breaks it into steps:

1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they need that reader to believe?
2. **Say what the author could see and what was out of view.**
3. **Notice word choices** where other words were available.
4. **State what the document can show and what it cannot.**

Here is a practice example on an invented document, not your letter. Suppose a mill owner writes to his stockholders: *"A few agitators led our loyal hands astray, and the mill stood idle three days."*

- **Position and interest:** He's an owner reporting to investors, so he wants to look in control.
- **Could see / out of view:** He could see the idle mill and the balance sheet, but probably not what workers said among themselves.
- **Word choice:** "agitators" and "loyal hands astray" frame the workers as followers, not people with grievances of their own.
- **Can show:** how owners talked about strikers and what he wanted investors to think.
- **Cannot show:** why the workers actually walked out.

Notice that the "can't show" column isn't a complaint that the source is biased. It marks the limit of what that source can tell us.
