**The method (Reader unit 3):**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. Note what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it cannot (others' motives, events the author didn't witness).

**Worked example on a parallel (invented) source:**
*A mill overseer writes to the owner: "The weavers walked out Tuesday. A few troublemakers stirred up the rest, who are sorry for it. I kept the looms oiled and stayed at my post."*

- **Position and interest:** The overseer reports to his employer and wants to look loyal and in control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the mill floor. He probably didn't see weavers' private conversations or what happened in their homes or meetings.
- **Word choice:** "Troublemakers" instead of "organizers," and "sorry for it" is a claim about feelings he can't directly know.
- **Can show:** What an overseer wanted his boss to believe, how management labeled workers, and what he felt he needed to prove.
- **Cannot show:** Why the weavers actually left, or whether most of them regretted it.

Notice that the "cannot show" points come straight from the gaps in what he could observe and from the claims he makes about other people.
