1. **Position and interest:** who made the document, for whom, and what did they want from it?
2. **Could see / couldn't see:** what was the author in a position to know, and what was out of view?
3. **Word choices:** where did the author pick one word when others were available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** what does the document reveal (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the people named), and what can't it tell you (the motives of the people described, events the author didn't witness)?

Here's a parallel example on a different source. Imagine a factory owner's letter to the town newspaper about a strike, saying "a few agitators misled our loyal employees." It can show how owners wanted the public to see the strike and what language they used to cast it. It can't show why workers walked out, since the owner is describing their motives secondhand. Set it against a worker's account of the same strike and you'd see whose voices are missing.
