**The task in three moves**
1. **Position and interest:** Who made this document, for whom, and what did they want the reader to believe or do?
2. **Vantage point:** What could the author see from where they stood, and what was out of view?
3. **Two columns, in prose:** What can the document show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded), and what can't it show (people's motives, events the author didn't witness)?

**A parallel example (not your source):** Imagine a mill owner's letter to his investors saying "a few agitators misled our loyal workers into walking out." It can show how owners described strikes and which workers they bothered to name. It can't show why the workers actually left, because the owner wasn't in their meetings. The word "agitators," chosen over "organizers" or "delegates," also tells you something about his stance. A worker's diary from the same week would fill in what the letter leaves out.
