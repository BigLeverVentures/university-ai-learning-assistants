Here's a worked example on a different, invented source so you can see the moves.

*Source: a principal's report to the school board after a student walkout.* "Order was restored by noon. The disturbance was led by three troublemakers from the senior class. Most students did not wish to take part."

- **Position and interest:** The principal is reporting to his employers, so he wants to look like he kept control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the schoolyard and who was disciplined. He couldn't see private conversations among students or why they walked out.
- **Word choice:** "Troublemakers" instead of "organizers," and "disturbance" instead of "protest," both frame the event as misbehavior.
- **Can show:** How the principal wanted the board to see events, the language authority used, and which students he knew by name.
- **Cannot show:** The students' real motives, or whether "most" really didn't want to participate.

Written as prose, that becomes a short paragraph covering perspective, what it shows, and what it can't.
