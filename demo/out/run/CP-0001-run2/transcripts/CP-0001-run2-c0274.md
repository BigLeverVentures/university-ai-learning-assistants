**The method (Reader unit 3)**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who made this, for whom, and to do what?
2. Ask what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot. Your 150 words are essentially this two-column list, written in prose.

**Practice example (invented, not your document)**
Imagine a mill owner's notice to his stockholders: *"The disturbance was confined to a handful of agitators. The great body of our hands remained loyal, and the mill stood idle only through intimidation."*

- **Position and interest:** The owner is writing to investors and wants to protect their confidence and his own management.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the mill's output and the numbers who stayed away. He probably could not see what workers said to each other or why they stayed out.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" and "intimidation" instead of "organizers" and "solidarity."
- **Can show:** How an owner framed a strike for investors, and what he wanted them to believe.
- **Cannot show:** Why workers actually stopped, or whether most of them were loyal.

Notice that the claim "the great body remained loyal" is the author's *assertion*. It is evidence of what he wanted believed, not proof of what workers felt.
