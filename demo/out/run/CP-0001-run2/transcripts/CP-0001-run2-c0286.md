2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck, and it usually means the task is less mysterious than it feels. Unit 3 of the Reader frames it as a **two-column list, written in prose**:

1. **Position and interest:** who made this, for whom, and what did they want the reader to believe or do?

3. **Word choices:** places where the author picked one word over another available one.
4. **Can show / cannot show:** what the document reveals (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, whose names were recorded) and what it can't (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness).

**Practice example (not your document).** Imagine an invented line from a mill owner's letter to his investors: *"A few agitators misled our loyal hands into abandoning their looms."*

- **Word choices:** "agitators" and "misled" rather than "organizers" and "persuaded." These words treat the workers as having no reasons of their own.
- **Can show:** how owners talked about strikers and what he wanted investors to believe.
- **Cannot show:** why the workers actually left, since no worker speaks.

That's the move: each point ties to specific words and then separates what the source *shows* from what we'd have to *infer*.

6. **assistant**: Yes, it matters a lot. The Reader's opening line says every document is made "by someone, for someone, to do something." Audience shapes three things:

- **What gets included.** A writer picks details the reader needs or will respond to. A report to a boss will stress what the writer did right.
- **What gets left out.** Writers skip what the reader already knows, what would look bad, or what they don't think matters.
