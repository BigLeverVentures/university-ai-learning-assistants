4. **assistant**: Here's a worked example on a different, made-up source so you can see the moves without it being your answer.

**Parallel source (invented):** A mill manager writes to the mill's owners after a walkout. He says "a handful of agitators misled the hands," that "the loyal workers were intimidated," and that he "kept the boilers running."

**Step 1: Author's position and interest.** He is the manager, writing to the owners, who pay him and can dismiss him. He wants them to see him as in control and not at fault.

**Step 2: What he could and couldn't see.** He could see who walked out, who stayed, and the state of the machinery. He could not see what was said at workers' meetings, or why individual workers made their choices.

**Step 3: Word choices.** He wrote "agitators" where "organizers" or "spokesmen" were available. He wrote "misled," which assumes the workers couldn't think for themselves. Those choices show his view, and they are evidence of that view. Whether the workers were actually "misled" is only his inference.

**Step 4: Can show / cannot show.**
- *Can show:* how a manager described a strike to his bosses, the language of authority, and which people he bothered to name.
- *Cannot show:* what the workers wanted, why they acted, or what happened where he wasn't.

Notice that the "can show" column is about the author and his world, not just the strike. That's the against-the-grain move in unit 3.
