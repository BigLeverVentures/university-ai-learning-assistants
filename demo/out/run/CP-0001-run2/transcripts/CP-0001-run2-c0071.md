**What the worksheet asks, in plain terms**

The Reader describes evaluating a source as a two-column list written in prose:

- **Perspective:** Who is the author, what is their position, and what do they want their reader to believe or do? A source is made by someone, for someone, to do something.
- **What it can show:** The author's view, the vocabulary of authority they use, and the names they recorded. It can also show things the author didn't mean to reveal, like what they assumed their reader wanted to hear.
- **What it cannot show:** Things out of the author's view, such as the motives of the people he describes or events where he wasn't present.

**A tiny parallel, not your document:** Imagine a shopkeeper writes to the mayor saying, "Rowdy youths wrecked my window." It shows what the shopkeeper blames and fears. It can't show why the youths were there or what they would say happened.
