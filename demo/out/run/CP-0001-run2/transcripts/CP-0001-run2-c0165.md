**What the worksheet is asking (Reader, unit 3)**
Every document was made by someone, for someone, to do something. So you evaluate it in three moves:
1. **Position and interest:** who wrote it, to whom, and what they wanted their reader to believe or do.
2. **Vantage point:** what the author could see from where they stood, and what was out of view.
3. **Two columns, written as prose:** what the document *can* show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it *cannot* show (the motives of the people described, anything that happened where the author wasn't).

The Reader also says to notice word choices, where the author picked one word when another was available, like "ringleaders" versus "spokesmen."

**Practice example (invented, not your assignment)**
Suppose a mill owner writes to his investors: *"A few agitators from outside the town stirred up my loyal hands, who walked out against their wishes. The mill is secure."*
- **Position and interest:** he's writing to people who hold his money, so he wants to look in control.
- **Vantage point:** he can see the mill gates and who is on the payroll. He can't see what workers said to each other at home or in meetings.
- **Word choices:** "agitators" and "loyal hands" frame the strike as outside meddling.
- **Can show:** how an owner explained a strike to investors, and what he wanted them to believe.
- **Cannot show:** why the workers actually walked out, or whether they were "loyal."

Notice that the analysis is about what the document reveals, not just whether it's true or false.
