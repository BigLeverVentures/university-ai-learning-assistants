**The steps (from Reader unit 3)**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. What could the author see from that position, and what was out of view?
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the source can show and what it cannot.

**Parallel example (invented, not your assignment)**
Say a harbor master writes to the town council: *"The fishermen refused to unload on Tuesday. A few agitators from out of town stirred them up. The catch spoiled."*

- **Position and interest:** He's an official reporting upward, probably wanting to look in control and not at fault.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the empty dock and the spoiled fish. He probably wasn't at the fishermen's meetings, so he can't know why they refused.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" rather than "organizers" or "neighbors" frames the cause as outsiders.
- **Can show:** how an official explained a work stoppage, what vocabulary authority used, and that the spoilage mattered to him. **Cannot show:** the fishermen's own reasons, or what was said at their meetings.

In prose, that becomes a short paragraph covering perspective, then "can show," then "cannot show."
