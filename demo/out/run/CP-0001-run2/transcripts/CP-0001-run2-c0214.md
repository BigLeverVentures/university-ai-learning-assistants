**The method:** every document was made by someone, for someone, to do something.
1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available (the Reader's example is "ringleaders" vs. "spokesmen").
4. State what the source can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it cannot (others' motives, events the author didn't witness).

**Practice example (invented, not your assignment):** Imagine a mill owner writing to his business partner: *"The weavers walked out Tuesday. Outside agitators had been at the gates, and I am sure most hands wished to stay."*
- **Perspective:** an owner writing to a partner, with money and reputation at stake.
- **Could see:** the empty looms, the gates, who was on the payroll. **Couldn't see:** what the weavers said to each other, or why they left.
- **Word choice:** "agitators" frames the strikers as manipulated, and "I am sure" is a claim, not evidence.
- **Can show:** how an owner explained a strike to his peers. **Cannot show:** what the weavers wanted, or whether most really wished to stay.

Notice that the last two parts separate what the text shows from what the author merely asserts.
