**The method (Reader unit 3):**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who wrote it, to whom, and what they wanted.
2. Ask what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

**Parallel example (invented, not your source):** A lighthouse keeper writes to the harbor board after a shipwreck: "The captain ignored my signal and drove his vessel onto the rocks. The crew was drunk."

- *Position and interest:* The keeper is an employee writing to his superiors, and he has reason to show he did his job.
- *Could see / couldn't see:* He saw the ship's approach from shore. He couldn't see conditions on deck, so "the crew was drunk" is a claim he had no way to witness.
- *Word choice:* "Ignored" assumes the captain saw and understood the signal. "Drove" assumes intent. Other words, like "failed to answer" or "was carried onto," were available.
- *Can show:* the keeper's account, what he thought the board would accept as blame, and that he logged the signal.
- *Cannot show:* the captain's reasons, or what happened on the ship.

Notice that the last step is what a worksheet entry turns into prose: a view of what the source shows, and a clear limit on what it doesn't.
