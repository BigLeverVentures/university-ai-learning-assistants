2. **assistant**: You're not missing something obvious. A list only "shows" something once you ask it questions. Reader unit 5 gives you two:

- **Down the page:** who appears, how often, and for what?
- **Across time:** where do entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening then?

The unit also asks you to keep three things apart: what the record was *for*, what it *shows* about discipline, and what it is *silent* about.

Here's a worked example on a made-up record, so you can see the moves without it touching your worksheet.

**Parallel example: a dormitory sign-in book, March 1923 (invented)**

> Mar 3, Hale, in at 11:40 pm, 10c. Mar 4, Pryor, in at 11:15 pm, 10c. Mar 9, Hale, in at midnight, 25c. Mar 15, Hale, in at 11:30 pm, 10c. Mar 16-22, no entries. Mar 30, Pryor, in at 11:20 pm, 10c.

- **What it was for:** The warden kept it to enforce a curfew and collect fines. It wasn't meant to describe student life.
- **Down the page:** There are only two names, and one appears three times. The fine rises for a later arrival. So the rule has a scale, and the enforcement looks uneven or targeted.
- **Across time:** A week of silence. That could mean everyone behaved, or the warden was away, or the dorm was closed for a holiday. I'd have to find out, not assume.
- **What it can't tell me:** Who slipped in unnoticed, who was let off, or what Hale thought of the fines.

Notice that each claim stays tied to something actually on the page, and each limit is stated as a limit, not a guess.
