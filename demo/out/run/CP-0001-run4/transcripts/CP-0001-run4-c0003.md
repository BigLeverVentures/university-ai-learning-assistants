1. **student**: It's just a list of names and amounts. I don't understand how a list is supposed to "show" anything about discipline. What am I missing?
2. **assistant**: You're not missing anything obvious. A list doesn't "show" anything until you ask it questions. Reader unit 5 gives you two:

- **Down the page:** who appears, how often, and for what?
- **Across time:** where do entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening then?

Here's a worked example on a made-up source, so you can see the moves without it being your page.

**Parallel example: a village school's detention book, one term (invented)**
*Entries: "Oct 3, Pell, talking, 10 min." "Oct 4, Marsh, no slate, 10 min." "Oct 9, Pell, talking, 20 min." "Oct 10, Pell, talking, 20 min." Then nothing from Oct 14 to Oct 25. Nov 1, Marsh, late, 10 min.*

- **Down the page:** the school punished talking, missing equipment, and lateness. Pell appears three times, so either Pell was singled out or the rule was enforced unevenly. The penalty rose from 10 to 20 minutes, which suggests escalation for repeat offences.
- **Across time:** the gap from Oct 14 to 25 is a fact to explain. Maybe the school was closed for harvest, or the teacher was ill. Without other evidence, you can't say which, but you can say the gap exists and name possible explanations.
- **What it can't show:** talking that went unnoticed, offences the teacher forgave, or what Pell thought about it.

So the list shows the rules enforced, who got caught, how often, and when enforcement stopped. It's silent on everything that never got written down. The Reader also asks you to keep three things apart: what the record was *for*, what it shows, and what it's silent about.
