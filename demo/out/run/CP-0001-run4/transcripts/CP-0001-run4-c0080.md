2. **assistant**: Good question, and you're not missing something obvious. A list only "shows" something once you ask what it was made for and read it in a particular way. Reader unit 5 gives a method: read **down the page** (who appears, how often, for what) and **across time** (where entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening then). Then keep three things apart: what the record was for, what it shows, and what it's silent about.

Here's a practice example on something unrelated to your excerpt, a school detention book:

> *Mon: Lee, talking, 1 period. Mon: Park, late, 1 period. Tue: Lee, talking, 2 periods. Wed: Lee, phone out, 2 periods. Thu: Park, late, 1 period. Fri: [no entries].*

- **Down the page:** The school enforces rules on talking, lateness, and phones. Lee appears three times, Park twice. The penalty grows for Lee's repeat offences, so the rules are applied with some escalation.
- **Across time:** Friday is blank. That could mean perfect behavior, but it could also mean a half day, an assembly, or a teacher who was away. It's a fact to explain, not proof of nothing.
- **Silences:** The book can't tell us who talked without being caught, which warnings were given informally, or what Lee thought of the punishments.

Notice that none of those claims needed the book to "tell a story." The pattern in the list is the evidence, and the limits come from what the clerk was required to write down.
