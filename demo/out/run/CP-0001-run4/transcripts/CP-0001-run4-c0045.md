**Parallel example: a school detention book, 1950s**

Imagine a page that reads: *Mon: Hill, talking, 1 hr. Tue: Hill, late, 30 min. Wed: Park, chewing gum, 30 min. Thu: Hill, talking, 1 hr. Fri: [no entries].*

- **What it was for:** The teacher kept it to track punishments and have proof of them.
- **What it shows about discipline:** Talking, lateness, and gum were all punishable. Punishments were set by the offence, not left to the moment. Hill is caught more often than anyone else. That could mean Hill misbehaved more, or that the teacher watched Hill more closely. The page can't tell you which, so that part is inference.
- **What it's silent about:** Every misbehavior that went unnoticed or got a warning instead. Why Hill acted up. What the students thought of the rules. And Friday: no entries might mean good behavior, but it might also mean a school assembly, a holiday, or a sick teacher. The silence needs explaining and shouldn't just be read as "nothing happened."

So a list can show discipline because it records what a workplace chose to punish, who got caught, and how often. Its limits are just as informative.
