1. **student**: It's just a list of names and amounts. I don't understand how a list is supposed to "show" anything about discipline. What am I missing?
2. **assistant**: You're not missing something obvious. A list only "shows" something once you ask it questions. Reader unit 5 gives you two:

- **Down the page:** who appears, how often, and for what?
- **Across time:** where do entries thin out or stop, and what else might have been happening then?

The Reader also says a ledger was written for a clerk's purpose, not to tell a story. That's why it can show the *rules enforced* but not what happened outside the clerk's view.

Here's a worked example on a parallel source, a made-up school detention book:

> *Mar 3, Pell J., talking, 1 hr. Mar 4, Pell J., talking, 1 hr. Mar 4, Voss R., no homework, 1 hr. Mar 6, Pell J., talking, 1 hr. Mar 10-14: no entries, "school closed, flood."*

- **Down the page:** Pell appears three times, always for talking. That suggests a rule about talking was enforced, and perhaps that Pell was singled out, or just chatty. The book can't say which.
- **Across time:** The blank week isn't "no misbehavior." The note says nobody was there to be punished. The silence has an explanation.
- **What it can't show:** whether other students talked and got away with it, or what Pell thought of the punishments.

So a "list" becomes evidence about discipline: which rules, who got caught, how often, and when enforcement stopped.
