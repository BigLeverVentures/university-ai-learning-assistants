**The idea:** A ledger doesn't tell you what people thought. But it does record what the owners *chose to police*, because a clerk only wrote down what he was required to write down. So a list of names and amounts shows you the rules being enforced, who got caught, and how often. It also has gaps, and the gaps matter too.

**Practice example (invented, not your assignment):** Imagine a bakery's "penalty book" for one week:

> Mon: Ines, late, 10c. Tue: Ines, late, 10c. Wed: Paul, dropped tray, 25c. Thu: Ines, late, 10c. Fri-Sat: no entries, note: "oven broken."

- **What it shows:** The bakery fined lateness and carelessness. Ines was caught three times, so either she was often late or she was watched closely. Those are two different explanations, and the book can't tell us which.
- **What it can't show:** Who was late but not caught, whether Ines had a reason, or what the workers thought of the fines.
- **The silence:** Fri-Sat is blank, but the note suggests it's because nobody was working, not because everyone behaved. A blank has an explanation.

That's the whole method: **what was it for, what does it show, what is it silent about.** Keep those three separate.
