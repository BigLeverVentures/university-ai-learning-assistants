2. **assistant**: You're not missing anything obvious. A list doesn't announce what it shows, so you have to learn to ask it questions. Reader unit 5 is written for exactly this situation.

Here's a parallel example on a made-up source, not your ledger. Imagine a school's detention book:

*Sept 3, Ali, late, 1 detention. Sept 3, Bea, chewing gum, 1 detention. Sept 10, Ali, late, 2 detentions. Sept 12, Cal, uniform, 1 detention. Sept 15-19: no entries (teachers' strike).*

Even this bare list can show a few things:
- **What the rules were:** lateness, gum, and uniform were punishable.
- **Who got caught, and how often:** Ali appears twice, and the penalty went up the second time.
- **Gaps:** the blank week isn't an empty page. It's a fact needing explanation.

It can't show:
- Who was late but not noticed.
- Which teachers let things slide.
- What Ali thought about it.

The unit's method is to read **down the page** (who, how often, for what) and **across time** (where entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening). Then keep three things apart: what the record was for, what it shows about discipline, and what it's silent about.

Now back to your page. Before deciding what it shows, ask who wrote it, for whom, and what they could or couldn't see. Looking at excerpt E, who made this record, and what do you think it was for?
