**Parallel example: Millbrook Bakery fines, March 1891**
Mar 3, Pell J., late, 10c. Mar 4, Pell J., late, 10c. Mar 6, Orr S., dropped tray, 25c. Mar 9, Pell J., late, 10c. Mar 15-17, no entries (margin: "oven repair").

1. **What was the record for?** The bakery needed a tally of penalties, so it recorded date, name, charge, and amount. It was not meant to describe the workers.
2. **Down the page (who, how often, what for).**

   - Penalties are set by offence: lateness costs less than breakage.

3. **Across time (where entries thin or stop).** Nothing is recorded Mar 15-17. The margin note suggests nobody was fined because the bakery wasn't running. The gap is a fact to explain, not a blank.
4. **What it's silent about.** It can't show who was late but unnoticed, or whether Pell was lazy or just watched more closely. It can't show what Pell thought of the fines.

Note that steps 2 and 4 are kept apart: what the page shows versus what it can't.
