2. **assistant**: That's a reasonable reaction, and the answer is in Reader unit 5: a list can show things precisely because nobody wrote it to tell a story. The reader's job is to ask it questions. The unit suggests reading a ledger in two directions:

- **Down the page:** who appears, how often, and for what?
- **Across time:** where do entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening then?

It also asks you to keep three things apart: what the record was for, what it shows, and what it's silent about.

Here's a practice example on an invented record, not your assignment. Imagine a town library's overdue-fines register for one month: names, dates, and amounts.

- **Purpose:** the library wrote it to collect money and keep track of who owed what, not to describe its patrons.
- **Down the page:** one name appears five times, and most fines are for the same offence. That shows which rule the library actually enforced and who got caught. It doesn't show who was late but never noticed.
- **Across time:** the entries stop for a week. That might mean everyone was punctual, but it might mean the library was closed. The silence is a fact to explain, not an absence of facts.

Notice that every claim is tied to something visible on the page, and the guesses are labeled as guesses.
