Here's a parallel example on different material. Imagine a school detention book:

*Mon: Cho, late, 1 period. Mon: Alvarez, chewing gum, 1 period. Wed: Cho, late, 1 period. Fri: [no entries].*

Even this bare list tells you some things:
- **What the school enforced:** lateness and gum were punishable, so those were rules.
- **Who got caught, and how often:** Cho appears twice.
- **What the gaps might mean:** an empty Friday could mean perfect behavior, or it could mean the school was closed.

It can't tell you who chewed gum without being noticed, which offences a teacher let slide, or what Cho thought of the punishment. The unit's advice is to keep three things apart: what the record was for, what it shows, and what it's silent about. It also suggests reading in two directions: down the page (who, how often, for what) and across time (where entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening then).
