2. **assistant**: Good question, and you're not missing a trick. A list only "shows" something once you ask what it was made for and then read it for patterns. The Reader's unit 5 calls this reading a ledger in two directions: down the page and across time.

Here's a practice example on a made-up document, not your excerpt.

*A college library fines book, one March:*
- Mar 2, Hale, overdue, 5c
- Mar 5, Hale, overdue, 5c
- Mar 9, Hale, overdue, 5c
- Mar 10, Ruiz, damaged book, 25c
- Mar 15-22, no entries, with a note: "library closed, flood"

- **Down the page:** the library enforced rules about overdue and damaged books, and one name keeps recurring. That tells us about what was policed and who got caught.
- **Across time:** the week with no entries doesn't mean everyone behaved. The note shows nobody could be fined because the library was shut. The silence has an explanation.
- **What it can't tell us:** who returned books late without being noticed, whether Hale was excused sometimes, or what Hale thought about the fines.

A list of names becomes evidence when you ask what the clerk had to record, what patterns appear, and what the record leaves out.
