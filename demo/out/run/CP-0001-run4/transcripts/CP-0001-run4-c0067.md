Imagine a school's detention book: date, pupil, offence, penalty. Nobody wrote it to tell a story, but you can read it two ways, as Reader unit 5 suggests:

- **Down the page:** Which rules got enforced (lateness? talking back?), who appears again and again, and how harsh the penalties were. That tells you what the school treated as punishable and who got caught.
- **Across time:** Where do entries thin out or stop? A blank week might mean good behavior, or it might mean the school was closed. A silence is a fact to be explained, not an absence of facts.

The book can't tell you who misbehaved without being noticed, which offences a teacher let slide, or what the pupils thought of the rules. A good analysis keeps three things apart: what the record was for, what it shows, and what it's silent about.
