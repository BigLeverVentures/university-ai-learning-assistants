**The distinction, using the Wrenfield reader (unit 2):**
- **Claim:** what the source asserts. "The strikers were misled by outside agitators" is a claim.
- **Evidence:** what the source shows or reports that you could check against other sources, like a time, a name, or something seen.
- **Inference:** the step *you* take from evidence toward a claim. It belongs to you, not the document.

So your instinct that "outside agitators caused the strike" is the evidence runs into a problem. That's the editorial's conclusion, just reworded. An editorial states its conclusions in the voice of fact, so it's easy to mistake them for evidence. The test is to ask what the editor would have needed to *see* to know that, and whether the text shows it.

**A tiny parallel example, not from your materials:** Suppose a newspaper says, "The fire was set by a rival baker." The claim is that the baker set the fire. A piece of evidence might be "a man was seen leaving the alley before the smoke appeared." The inference, which is the reader's, is that the man might have been connected to the fire, though that doesn't prove who he was.
