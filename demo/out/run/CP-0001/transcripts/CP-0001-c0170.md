**The steps (from the Reader):**
1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

**Worked example (invented, not your source):**
A harbor clerk writes to the company manager: *"The dockhands walked off at noon. Their leader, Marsh, a loud man of no standing, kept them from the gangways. I believe most would have stayed at work. The cargo is secure."*

1. **Position and interest:** He's a clerk reporting to his boss, and he wants to look competent and loyal.
2. **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the gangways and who walked off. He couldn't see private conversations among the dockhands or what they were deciding.
3. **Word choices:** "Leader" and "kept them from" instead of "spokesman" and "asked them to wait." Both choices frame the walkout as one man's doing.
4. **Can show:** how a clerk framed a walkout for a manager, the vocabulary of authority, and that Marsh's name was recorded. **Cannot show:** why the dockhands actually left, or whether "most would have stayed" is true, since that is a claim about other people's minds.

Notice the final evaluation would turn those four notes into prose: perspective, what it can show, what it cannot.
