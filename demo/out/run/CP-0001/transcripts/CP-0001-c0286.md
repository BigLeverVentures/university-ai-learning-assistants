**The distinction:**
- **Claim:** what the source asserts, in its own voice. ("The strikers were misled by outside agitators" is one.)
- **Evidence:** something the source reports that you could check against other sources, like a date, a name, or something seen or promised.
- **Inference:** a step *you* take from the evidence. It isn't in the document, so you mark it as yours.

Your first answer put the claim in the evidence slot. The editorial believes the agitators caused the strike, but that belief is its conclusion, not something it shows.

**Practice example (invented mill owner's notice, not your source):**
> "The walkout was stirred up by a stranger who spoke to the hands at the gate. Our people had no complaint. Wages were paid in full on Saturday."

- *Claim, in the owner's words:* "The walkout was stirred up by a stranger."
- *Evidence, in my words:* The owner reports that a stranger spoke at the gate and that wages were paid on Saturday. Both could be checked against other accounts or pay records.
- *Inference (mine):* A stranger speaking at the gate doesn't show he caused the walkout. The owner may be pointing to him to avoid discussing conditions inside the mill.

Notice that the evidence is smaller and more concrete than the claim, and the inference points out the gap between them.
