**The method (from Reader unit 3)**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who wrote it, to whom, and what they wanted.
2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot, in prose.

The "can show / cannot show" part is the heart of it. A source can be useful for something it never meant to tell you.

**A worked example on a different source (not your assignment)**

Imagine an invented letter from a lighthouse keeper to a district inspector after a storm. It says the lamp was kept burning all night, that the supply boat is weeks late, and that "the assistant, a lazy fellow, slept through the worst of it."

- *Position and interest:* The keeper is writing to his boss, so he wants to look dutiful and to get supplies.
- *Could see / couldn't see:* He saw the lamp and the storm at his station. He couldn't see the wrecks offshore or what his assistant was actually doing while he was elsewhere.
- *Word choice:* "Lazy fellow" is a judgment, not a description. "Kept burning" stresses his own effort.
- *Can show:* How a keeper presented his duty to an inspector, what he thought the inspector cared about, and that supplies were a problem.
- *Cannot show:* Whether the assistant really slept, or how bad the storm was beyond the keeper's own account.

Notice that the final answer is the two lists turned into a few sentences.
