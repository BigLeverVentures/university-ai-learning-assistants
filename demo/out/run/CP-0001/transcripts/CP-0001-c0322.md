**Worked analysis**

*What was it for?* The gatekeeper kept it to record infractions and money owed. The mill's management was the audience, not the workers or later readers.

*What it shows about discipline (evidence):*
- Lateness was the most common offence, and it carried a small, fixed fine.
- Drunkenness cost five times as much, so the mill treated it as more serious.
- Pell appears four times, and someone added a note to send him to the overseer. That suggests (inference) repeated fines could lead to escalation beyond the fine itself.

*What it is silent about:*
- Everyone who was late but not caught, or caught and forgiven.
- Why Pell was late, and what he thought of the fines.
- Whether the fines changed anyone's behavior.

*The gap, Mar 14-18:* No entries doesn't mean everyone behaved. The margin note says the mill was stopped, so nobody was working to be fined. The silence has an explanation, and the explanation is in the document itself.

*Keeping the three apart:* the record shows rules and enforcement, and it says nothing about motives or the experience of the workers.
