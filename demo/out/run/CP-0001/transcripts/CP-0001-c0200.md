4. **assistant**: Here's a worked example on a made-up source, so you can see the method without it being your letter.

**Parallel source (invented):** A mill owner writes to his investors after a walkout. He says "a handful of agitators misled the hands," that the mill "stands idle through no fault of management," and that "the loyal workers" want to return.

**Step 1: Author's position and interest.** He owns the mill and is writing to people who have money at stake. He wants them to believe he's in control and not to blame.

**Step 2: What he could and couldn't see.**
- *Could see:* the idle machinery, the financial losses, who showed up at the gate.
- *Out of view:* what workers said to each other, why they walked out, and whether the "loyal workers" were loyal or just afraid.

**Step 3: Word choices.** He writes "agitators" where he could have written "organizers" or "spokesmen," and "hands" where he could have written "workers." Those choices cast the strikers as troublemakers and the workers as tools.

**Step 4: Can show / cannot show.**
- *Can show:* how an owner explained a strike to investors, the vocabulary of authority, and which events he thought worth reporting.
- *Cannot show:* the strikers' real motives, or whether most workers supported the strike.

Notice that "he wanted to look blameless" is an **inference**. The evidence is the words "through no fault of management."
