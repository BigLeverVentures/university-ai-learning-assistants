**The steps**
1. **Author's position and interest:** Who is writing, to whom, and what might they want?
2. **What they could see vs. what was out of view:** Where was the author standing, literally and socially?
3. **Word choices:** Where did the author pick one word when others were available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What does the document reveal (even unintentionally), and what can't it tell you?

**A worked example on a made-up parallel source (not your assignment)**
Imagine a mill overseer writes to the owner: *"The weavers walked out Tuesday. A few agitators stirred up the rest, who are sorry for it. I kept the looms oiled."*

- *Position and interest:* The overseer is reporting to his boss and wants to look competent and in control.
- *Seen vs. unseen:* He saw the walkout begin. He probably didn't hear the weavers' private conversations or their reasons.
- *Word choice:* "Agitators" rather than "organizers," and "walked out" rather than "refused to work." These words frame the weavers as misled.
- *Can show:* How an overseer talked about workers, what he wanted the owner to believe, and what he did during the walkout. *Cannot show:* why the weavers acted, or whether the "sorry for it" claim is true.
