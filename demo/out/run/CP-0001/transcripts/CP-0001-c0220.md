**Your question first:** "Outside agitators caused the strike" is the editorial's *claim*, not its evidence. An editorial states its conclusions in the voice of fact, so it's easy to mistake one for the other. Unit 2 of the Reader describes the three categories this way:

- **Claim:** what the source asserts, in its own words.
- **Evidence:** something the source reports that a reader could check against other sources, like a date, a name, or a thing seen.
- **Inference:** a step *you* take from the evidence toward a conclusion. It belongs to you, not the document.

**Parallel example (not your source):** Imagine a mill owner's letter saying, "My weavers were stirred up by a union man from the city, and they walked out at noon."

- *Claim:* "stirred up by a union man from the city."
- *Evidence:* the walkout happened at noon. That is something that could be checked, and the owner could have seen it himself.
- *Inference (mine):* the owner may be blaming an outsider so the weavers' own grievances don't need an answer. That's my reading, and the letter doesn't say it.

Notice that the claim was not used as its own evidence. The evidence is a smaller, checkable detail.
