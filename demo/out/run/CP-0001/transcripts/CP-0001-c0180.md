**The method, in steps**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who wrote it, to whom, and what they wanted the reader to believe or do.
2. List what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it cannot (others' motives, events the author didn't witness).

Your 150 words are basically that two-column list, written as prose.

**Parallel example (invented, not your assignment)**
Imagine a letter from a mill overseer to the owner after a walkout of weavers: *"The weavers left their looms at noon. The agitators, Mrs. Cole and Miss Hart, misled the girls. I have kept the boiler running."*

- **Position and interest:** The overseer is writing to his employer, and his job depends on appearing in control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see who left and when, and what happened at the mill. He probably couldn't see the weavers' private discussions or their reasons.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" and "misled" rather than "organizers" or "persuaded." These words treat the workers as followers, not decision-makers.
- **Can show:** How an overseer framed a strike for the owner, and which workers he named. **Cannot show:** Why the weavers actually left, or whether they were "misled."

Notice that each claim is tied to something in the text and to the author's position.
