**The method:** The unit treats evaluating a source as a two-column list written in prose.

3. Notice word choices where other words were available (the Reader's examples are "ringleaders" vs. "spokesmen" and "abandoned" vs. "left").

**A practice example (not your document):** Imagine a mill owner's notice to the town saying "agitators from outside stirred up our loyal hands, who were then forced from their looms."
- **Position and interest:** The owner wants the town's sympathy and wants to protect his reputation.
- **What he could see:** His own mill and the strikers who left it.
- **What was out of view:** The workers' meetings, their grievances, and their reasons.
- **Word choices:** "Agitators" and "forced" are judgments, not neutral descriptions.
- **What it can show:** How an owner wanted the strike understood, and the language of authority.
- **What it can't show:** What the workers wanted, or whether they were actually forced.

Notice that the source is useful even though it's one-sided. You're asking what it reveals, not whether it's "true" or "false."
