**What the task asks (from Reader unit 3):** You're not summarizing the letter. You're asking what kind of evidence it is. The Reader gives a sequence:

1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

**Worked example on invented material (not your assignment):**

> *Harbor master's report to the port board, 1881:* "The stevedores refused the night shift on Thursday. The trouble was stirred up by a few hotheads, notably Bryce, who has no stake in the Company. Most of the men are loyal and would work if the wages were paid on time. I was at the office all evening and can report the wharf was quiet."

- **Position and interest:** He's a manager reporting upward, and he wants the board to see him as in control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the wharf from the office, but he didn't hear what the men said to each other. His claim about "most of the men" is a guess.
- **Word choices:** "Stirred up" and "hotheads" make the trouble sound like it came from outside agitators, where "organized" or "spoke for the others" would have sounded different.
- **Can show:** how a manager framed the dispute, which vocabulary he used, and which name he recorded. **Cannot show:** why the men refused, or what happened away from his view.

Notice that the final step is a pair of claims, each tied to something in the text. That's the shape your 150 words should take, in prose.
