2. **assistant**: That's a common stuck point, and the assignment is less mysterious than it sounds. Unit 3 of the Reader gives you a four-step method, and your 150 words are basically the result of those steps written as prose:

1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want?
2. **List what they could see and what was out of view.** What did their position let them observe, and what couldn't they know?
3. **Notice word choices.** Where did they pick one word when another was available?
4. **State what the source can show and what it cannot.** Typically it shows the author's view, the language of authority, and whom they bothered to name. It can't show other people's motives or events the author didn't witness.

Here's a worked example on a made-up source, not yours:

*A dock supervisor writes to the harbor board after dockhands refuse a night shift. He says "a few troublemakers stirred up the crew."*

- **Position and interest:** He's a manager reporting upward, so he wants to look in control.
- **Seen and unseen:** He saw who stood at the gate. He didn't hear what was said at the workers' own meetings.
- **Word choice:** "Troublemakers" frames the dispute as a discipline problem, where "organizers" or "representatives" would frame it differently.
- **Can and can't show:** It shows how managers described dissent and which names he recorded. It can't show why the crew actually refused.
