Here's a worked example on a different, invented source, so you can see the moves.

*Source: a mill overseer's letter to the owner, 1840s, reporting that the weavers walked out. He calls them "deserters," blames "agitators from the town," and says "the hands" were content until then.*

- **Position and interest:** The overseer answers to the owner, and a walkout on his watch could cost him his job. He has a reason to make it look like outside troublemakers, not bad management.
- **What he could see:** the mill floor and who was absent. **What was out of view:** what the weavers discussed among themselves and what they wanted.
- **Word choice:** "Deserters" treats leaving as disloyalty, where "walked out" would be neutral. "The hands" erases individuals.
- **Can show:** how an overseer framed a walkout for his employer, and what vocabulary of authority he used. **Cannot show:** why the weavers actually left, or whether they were content.
