> *Harlow Mill, weaving room. Deductions from pay, March. Kept by overlooker S. Marsh.*
> *Mar 3, Pike J., spoiled cloth, 10c. Mar 4, Pike J., spoiled cloth, 10c. Mar 4, Lund E., broken shuttle, 15c. Mar 9, Pike J., spoiled cloth, 10c. Mar 14-16, no entries; margin: "water low." Mar 17, Lund E., spoiled cloth, 10c.*

**1. Purpose and perspective first.** An overlooker kept this, probably for the mill office, so wages could be adjusted. He recorded only what he noticed and was required to write down.

**2. Down the page (evidence).** Pike appears three times, always for spoiled cloth. Lund appears twice, for different reasons. Deductions are 10c or 15c.

**3. What that supports (inference, labeled).** *Inference:* the mill treated spoiled cloth as a chargeable offence, since the rule is enforced in the entries themselves. **4. Across time.** Nothing is recorded Mar 14-16, and the margin says "water low." *Inference:* the looms may have stopped, so no entries doesn't mean good behavior. The margin note is the evidence for that.

**5. What it can't show.** Whether the deductions were fair, what Pike thought, or what went unrecorded.

Notice that the structure is purpose, then what the page shows, then what it's silent about, with evidence and inference kept apart. That matches Reader unit 5's last sentence.
