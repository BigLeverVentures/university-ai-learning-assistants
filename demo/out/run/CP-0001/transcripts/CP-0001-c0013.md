**The method in four moves**
1. **Position and interest:** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want or need from the reader?
2. **Seen vs. unseen:** What could the author observe from where they stood, and what was out of view?
3. **Word choices:** Where did the author pick one word when others were available?
4. **Can show vs. cannot show:** What does the document reveal (the author's view, the language of authority, who got named), and what can't it tell us (the motives of the people described, events the author didn't witness)?

**Worked example on made-up material (not your letter)**

Imagine a harbor master's note to the port owner: *"The dockhands have deserted their posts. A few agitators, led by a sailor named Ortiz, have misled the rest. I have kept the cranes secure."*

- *Position and interest:* He's an employee reporting to his boss, so he wants to look in control and blameless.
- *Seen vs. unseen:* He saw the empty docks and the cranes, but probably not what was said in the workers' meetings.
- *Word choice:* "Deserted" and "agitators" frame the workers as disloyal, where "stopped work" or "organizers" would be more neutral.
- *Shows / can't show:* It shows how a manager described a walkout and that Ortiz's name was recorded. It can't show why the dockhands actually stopped work.

In prose, that becomes a short paragraph covering perspective, then what it shows, then what it can't.
