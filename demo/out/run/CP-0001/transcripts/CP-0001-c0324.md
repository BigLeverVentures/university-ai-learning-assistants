**Practice example (invented): a village school's tardy book, autumn term**

*Entries:* Sept 3, Hale J., 10 min. Sept 3, Pryce L., 5 min. Sept 10, Hale J., 15 min. Sept 17, Hale J., 20 min. Oct 1, Pryce L., 5 min. Oct 8-15: no entries; margin: "harvest, school closed."

**1. What was it for?** The schoolmaster kept it to document attendance for the school board. It was written to prove he enforced punctuality, not to explain why children were late.

**2. Down the page.** Hale appears four times and Pryce twice. The book shows that lateness was recorded by the minute, so punctuality was a rule someone cared about. It also shows who got written down. Hale was caught repeatedly, which suggests either that he was often late or that he was watched more closely. The book alone can't tell us which.

**3. Across time.** The entries stop for a week, and the margin explains why. That silence isn't an absence of lateness. The school was closed, so lateness couldn't be recorded.

**4. What it can't show.** It can't show why Hale was late (a long walk? farm chores?), which latecomers the master let slide, or what the children or parents thought of the rule.

Notice the three jobs in that analysis: what the record was for, what it shows about discipline, and what it's silent about. Reader unit 5 asks you to keep those three apart.
