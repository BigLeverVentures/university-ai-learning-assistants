**The method in short:** the worksheet asks you to treat the letter as something *someone made, for someone, to do something*. Your 150 words are basically a two-column list written as prose:
1. **Perspective:** who wrote it, from what position, and what did they want?
2. **What they could see vs. couldn't:** what was in view from where they stood?
3. **Word choices:** places where the author picked one word over another available one.
4. **Can show / cannot show:** what the document reveals (even unintentionally) and what it can't tell you.

**Worked example (invented, not your source):**
*A school inspector's report, 1890: "The master keeps good order. The children are backward in arithmetic, owing to the idleness of their parents. Attendance was thin on the day of my visit."*

- **Position and interest:** an outside official judging the school for his superiors, so he wants to look thorough and to place blame somewhere.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** one day's visit. He couldn't see why children were absent, or what home life was like.
- **Word choice:** "idleness" is a judgment where "work" or "poverty" might have been used. He may not have known the parents at all.
- **Can show:** what an inspector expected of families, and the vocabulary of authority. **Cannot show:** the parents' actual reasons, or what a typical school day looked like.

Notice that the "cannot show" part isn't "he might be biased, so ignore him." It's specific: what *this* author, from *this* spot, would have missed.
