2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck. The worksheet isn't asking you to summarize the letter. It asks you to treat it as something a particular person made, for a particular reader, for a reason. Reader unit 3 breaks that into steps:

1. **Position and interest:** who wrote it, to whom, and what they needed from that reader.
2. **What they could see and what was out of view** from where they stood.
3. **Word choices:** places where the author picked one word when others were available.
4. **Two columns, in prose:** what the document *can* show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it *cannot* show (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness).

Here's a quick practice example on an invented source, not yours. Imagine a mill owner writes to investors: *"A few agitators misled our hands, who have since drifted away from their posts."*

- **Position:** the owner is writing to people who care about profit and order, so he wants to look in control.
- **Word choice:** "agitators" and "misled" suggest the workers couldn't have had their own grievances. A worker might have written "organizers" and "walked out."
- **Can show:** how owners framed strikes to investors, and which people he chose to name.
- **Cannot show:** why the workers actually left, or what they said to one another.
