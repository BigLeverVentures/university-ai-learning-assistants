4. **State what the source can show and what it cannot.** The Reader calls this a two-column list written in prose.

Here's the method on a made-up parallel case, not your letter. Imagine a mill owner writing to a newspaper: "A few agitators misled our loyal workers, who left the floor only out of fear."

- **Position and interest:** The owner is writing to the public and wants to look like a fair employer.
- **Could see:** The mill's output, who showed up, and which names reached his office.
- **Out of view:** What was said at workers' meetings and why people walked out.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" and "misled" frame the workers as lacking their own reasons. "Spokesmen" and "persuaded" would frame them differently.
- **Can show:** How the owner wanted the strike understood, and what language he used for it.
- **Cannot show:** What the workers wanted or why they acted.

A strong entry covers both columns, and each point rests on specific words or details in the source. Your own inferences should be marked as inferences.
