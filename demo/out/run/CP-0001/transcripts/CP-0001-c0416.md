2. List what the author could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

Here is a **practice example on made-up material, not your letter**. Imagine a mill owner writes to his stockholders: *"A few agitators misled our loyal hands, who walked out against their will. The mill is secure."*

- **Position and interest:** He is answerable to investors and wants to look in control.
- **Could see / out of view:** He saw the mill gate and the financial losses. He didn't see what was said in workers' meetings or why individuals left.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" rather than "organizers," and "misled" rather than "persuaded."
- **Can show:** How an owner framed a strike for investors, and what he valued (security, loyalty).
- **Cannot show:** What the workers actually wanted, or whether they were "misled."

Notice that the last step separates what the source *shows* from what it only *claims*. That's the line between evidence and inference.
