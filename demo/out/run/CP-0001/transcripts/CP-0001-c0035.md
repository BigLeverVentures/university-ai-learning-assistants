**Example source (invented):** A hotel manager writes to the owner after the kitchen staff walk out. He says the cook "stirred up" the others, that most of the staff "were content," and that the dining room "remained in good order."

**Step 1: Author, audience, interest.** The manager answers to the owner, and his job depends on running the hotel well. He has a reason to present himself as in control and to put the blame on a few individuals.

**Step 2: What he could and couldn't see.** He could see who left and when, and what the dining room looked like. He probably couldn't see what the kitchen staff said to each other, or why they left.

**Step 3: Word choices.** "Stirred up" suggests the cook caused it. The staff might have said "organized" or "asked for better pay." "Content" is a claim about other people's feelings, and the manager can't know that directly.

**Step 4: Can show / cannot show.**
- *Can show:* how a manager wanted the owner to see events, the language of authority, and which employees he named.
- *Cannot show:* the staff's actual motives, or what happened out of his sight.

Notice that each point rests on specific words or on his position, and I labeled which claims are inference. For example, "he has a reason to blame individuals" is an inference from his job, not something the letter says outright.
