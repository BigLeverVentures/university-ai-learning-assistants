**Parallel source (invented):** A mill owner writes to the town newspaper: *"Our weavers were misled by outside agitators and have deserted their looms. I am confident most of them wish only to return to honest work."*

**Step 1: Provenance (who, for whom, what they could see)**
- *Who and for whom:* The mill owner wrote to the newspaper, so his audience is the public, not just his own managers. **Evidence:** "to the town newspaper."
- *Interest:* He wants readers to see the owner as reasonable and the strike as unjustified. **Inference:** He cares about his reputation and about pressure on the strikers.
- *Could see:* The mill floor, the empty looms, who was missing. *Probably couldn't see:* what the weavers said to each other at their own meetings, or why they walked out.

**Step 2: Word choice**
- "Deserted" rather than "walked out," and "agitators" rather than "organizers." **Evidence:** the words themselves. **Inference:** the choices cast the workers as disloyal and the outsiders as troublemakers.
- "I am confident most wish only to return" is a claim about other people's feelings. It tells us what he *hoped* readers would believe, not what the weavers wanted.

**Step 3: Can and can't show**
- *Can show:* how an owner described a strike publicly, what vocabulary he used, and what he wanted the town to think.
- *Can't show:* the weavers' real motives, or what happened where he wasn't.
