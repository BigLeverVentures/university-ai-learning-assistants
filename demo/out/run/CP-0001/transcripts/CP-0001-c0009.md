2. **assistant**: That's a common place to get stuck. The task is less "summarize the letter" and more "ask what kind of evidence this is." Reader unit 3 gives a four-step method:

1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. **What could the author see, and what was out of view?**
3. **Notice word choices** where other words were available.
4. **State what the document can show and what it cannot.**

Here's a worked example on made-up material, not your letter.

> *Note from a restaurant manager to the owner: "The kitchen staff walked out Friday during the dinner rush. I believe the dishwashers were put up to it by the new line cook. Most of the others seemed unhappy to go. I stayed and ran the grill myself."*

- **Position and interest:** The manager is reporting to his boss. He wants to look in control and not to blame.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the walkout itself. He didn't hear the staff's private conversations, so "put up to it" is a guess.
- **Word choices:** "Put up to it" suggests the workers couldn't have decided for themselves. "Walked out" could have been "left" or "protested."
- **Can show:** the manager's view, what he thought the owner wanted to hear, and which staff he singled out by role. **Cannot show:** why the staff actually left, or what happened out of his sight.
