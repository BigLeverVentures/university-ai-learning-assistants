**Why a list can "show" something:** A fines ledger exists because a workplace had rules and someone was paid to record when they were broken. So the list shows which rules were enforced, who got caught, and how often, even though nobody meant it to tell a story. It can't show everything, only what the clerk was required to write down.

**Practice example (invented, not your assignment):** Imagine a page from a bakery's "late book":

> March 3, Hale, 10 min., 5c. March 4, Hale, 5 min., 5c. March 4, Voss, 20 min., 10c. March 5-8, [no entries; note: "oven broken, closed"]. March 9, Hale, 10 min., 5c.

- **Down the page:** Hale appears three times, always for lateness, always a small fine. That tells us lateness was a punishable offence and that Hale was caught repeatedly.
- **Across time:** Entries stop March 5-8. The note explains why: the bakery was closed. No fines doesn't mean everyone behaved, it means no one was working.
- **What it can't show:** whether others were late and not caught, whether Hale had a reason, or what Hale thought of the fines.

Notice the three moves: what the record was for, what it shows, and what it's silent about.
