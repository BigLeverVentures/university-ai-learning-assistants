**What the entry is asking.** Every document is written by someone, for someone, to get something done. Evaluating a source means answering three questions about that:

1. **Perspective:** Who is the author, what is their position, and what do they want from their reader?
2. **What it can show:** What does the document tell us, even if the author didn't intend it? That includes the author's own view, the language of their position, and the names or details they recorded.
3. **What it cannot show:** What was out of the author's sight, or what is he only guessing at?

**A parallel example (not your source).** Imagine a store manager emailing the owner after a staff walkout: "Most of my staff didn't want to leave. The troublemakers are Alex and Sam." The manager's position is that he answers to the owner and wants to look in control. That email *can* show how he sees the walkout and which workers he knew by name. It *cannot* show why people walked out, because he's reporting what he believes about their motives, not what they said.
