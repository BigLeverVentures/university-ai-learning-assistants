**Practice example (invented):**
*"To the Mayor: The street lamps on Ash Row have been out for a week. I have asked the lamplighter twice. Honest residents are afraid to walk home, and only the rowdy element is pleased. Signed, a Ratepayer."*

1. **Position and interest:** An anonymous resident writing to an official. He wants the lamps fixed, and calling himself a "ratepayer" signals that he thinks he has a claim on the Mayor's attention.
2. **Could see / couldn't see:** He can see his own street and whether the lamplighter responded to him. He can't see the city budget or why the lamps are out.
3. **Word choices:** "Honest residents" versus "the rowdy element" sorts people into groups without evidence.
4. **Can show / cannot show:** It can show how one resident framed a complaint to win an official's sympathy. It cannot show how dangerous Ash Row actually was, or what the "rowdy" people wanted.

Notice that every point in the second half is tied to particular words in the note, and the inferences (like "he wants the Mayor's sympathy") are labeled as inferences.
