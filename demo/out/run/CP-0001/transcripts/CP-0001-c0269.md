**Practice source (invented):** A mill owner writes to his investors: *"The weavers have walked out. A few agitators from the neighboring town stirred up the otherwise contented hands. I have refused to meet any committee. Production will resume as soon as order is restored."*

**1. Author, audience, interest.** The author is the owner, writing to investors. He wants them to believe the situation is under control and that it isn't his fault.

**2. What he could and couldn't see.**
- He could see: the empty looms, who stayed away, and his own decisions.
- He couldn't see: what was said in the weavers' meetings, or why individual weavers walked out.

**3. Word choices.**
- *Evidence:* he writes "agitators" and "contented hands," where "organizers" or "workers" were available.
- *Inference:* he is shifting blame onto outsiders, and he may not have known what the weavers' grievances were.

**4. Can show / cannot show.**
- *Can show:* how an owner described a strike to investors, the vocabulary of authority, and that he refused to negotiate (he says so himself).
- *Cannot show:* whether the weavers were really contented, who the "agitators" were, or the workers' reasons.
