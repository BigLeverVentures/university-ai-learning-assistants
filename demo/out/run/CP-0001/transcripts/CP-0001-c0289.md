**The distinction:**
- **Claim:** what the author asserts or concludes ("the strikers were misled by outside agitators").
- **Evidence:** something the source reports that you could check against other sources, like a time, a place, a person, or a thing seen.
- **Inference:** the step *you* take from evidence toward a conclusion. It belongs to you, not the document.

Your restatement, "outside agitators caused the strike," is the claim again in different words. Evidence has to be something the author *points to* in support of the claim.

**Practice example (invented, not your assignment):**
> *From a mill owner's notice, 1890:* "The walkout was stirred up by a hired speaker from the city. A stranger addressed the hands at the east gate Tuesday morning, and the mill has paid full wages on time for six years."

- **Claim, in the notice's words:** the walkout "was stirred up by a hired speaker from the city."
- **Evidence, in my words:** a stranger spoke to workers at the east gate on Tuesday morning. That's a reported, checkable detail.
- **Inference, marked as mine:** the speaker may have influenced the workers. But being present isn't the same as causing the walkout, and the notice doesn't show what the workers themselves wanted.

Notice that the claim and the evidence are different kinds of statement. The evidence is a specific observation, while the claim is a conclusion drawn from it.
