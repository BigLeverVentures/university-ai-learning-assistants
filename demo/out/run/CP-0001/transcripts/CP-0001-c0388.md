2. **assistant**: That's a common reaction, and the Reader's unit 5 speaks to it directly: administrative records weren't written to tell a story, and that's what makes them useful. A list can show things if you know what to ask of it.

Here's a parallel example on invented material, a school detention book:

> *Mon: Lee, talking in class. Tue: Patel, late. Wed: Lee, late. Thu: Lee, talking. Fri: no entries (school closed for a holiday).*

- **What it shows about discipline:** which rules the school enforced (talking, lateness), who got caught, and how often. Lee shows up three times, so enforcement may have fallen unevenly.
- **What it can't show:** who talked without being noticed, which offences a teacher let slide, or what Lee thought about being punished.
- **The gaps:** the empty Friday isn't "no discipline." It's a fact with an explanation, and you'd need other evidence to find it.

Unit 5 describes this as reading in two directions: *down the page* (who, how often, for what) and *across time* (where entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening).
