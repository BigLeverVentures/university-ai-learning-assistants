**The three terms**
- **Claim:** what the source asserts about the world, often in the voice of fact.
- **Evidence:** what the source shows or reports that you could check against other sources, such as a date, a name, or something seen.
- **Inference:** the step *you* take from evidence toward a claim. It belongs to the reader, not the document.

Your sentence, "outside agitators caused the strike," treats the editorial's conclusion as if it were evidence. That's the mix-up the unit warns about. The editorial's conclusion is its **claim**.

**Parallel example (not your source)**
Imagine a mill owner's notice that says: "The walkout was stirred up by troublemakers from the next county. Three men I didn't recognize were at the gate at dawn."

- *Claim, in the owner's words:* "stirred up by troublemakers from the next county."
- *Evidence, in my words:* the owner reports seeing three unfamiliar men at the gate at dawn. That's an observation someone could check.
- *Inference, marked as mine:* I might infer the owner assumed that unfamiliar men meant outsiders with an agenda. But the notice doesn't show what the men said or did.

Notice that the evidence is smaller and more concrete than the claim, and the inference is the gap between them.
