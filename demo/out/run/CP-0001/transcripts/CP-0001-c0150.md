**3. Inference (what I'm concluding, and how confident)**
- *Inference:* He presents himself as a reluctant, put-upon owner. His mention of "daughters" and his low returns suggest he wants sympathy and wants to show he isn't profiteering. This rests on tone and word choice, so it is moderately strong.
- *Inference:* The standpipe offer is a bargaining move rather than a simple concession, because it is conditional on withdrawing the notice. That is strong, since the condition is stated.
- *Inference:* His claim that tenants block the drains is his account only. The letter alone can't confirm or refute it, so I'd treat it as a claim to test against other sources.
- *What the document can't tell me:* what the tenants would say, or what the inspector actually found.

**4. Use in a narrative:** This is a landlord's self-justification. It shows how owners framed responsibility, but it is weak evidence for actual conditions.
