## Practice example: B-3 (not for citation)

**1. Provenance and perspective**
- **Who:** G. Ambrose, a property owner, writing about his own court. The letter itself says he bought it in 1879 "as an investment for my daughters."
- **For whom:** The town council, which has served a notice on him.
- **When:** 1886, after an inspection.
- **What he could and couldn't see:** He knows his finances and his repair history. He doesn't speak for the tenants, and he has a strong reason to shape the story, since he wants the notice withdrawn.

**2. Evidence vs. inference**

| Claim | Evidence (the words) | Inference (my reading) |
|---|---|---|
| He frames the problem as the tenants' fault | "they are stopped because the tenants throw refuse into them" | He is deflecting responsibility, though he may also believe it. |
| He is defending his returns | "less than four per cent in any year" | He is signaling that costly repairs aren't worth it to him. |
| He is open to a limited concession | "willing to put in a second standpipe... if the Council will withdraw the notice" | This is a bargain, not an admission of fault. |

**3. What the source can and can't tell us**
- It tells us how an owner justified himself to officials.
- It can't tell us how many people lived in the court or what the tenants thought. His claim of "families of nine in a single room" is his own assertion.

**4. Sample annotation (two sentences)**
*A landlord's 1886 letter to the council defends his property against a sanitary notice by blaming tenants and the age of the building. It is useful for seeing how owners argued against regulation, but it is one-sided on conditions and silent on the tenants' own views.*
