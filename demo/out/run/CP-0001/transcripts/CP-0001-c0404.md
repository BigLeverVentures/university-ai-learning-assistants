2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point, so let's make the task more concrete. The Reader's unit 3 method has four moves:

1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what they could see from that position and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it can't.

Here's a practice example on a made-up source, not your assigned letter.

**Practice source (invented):** A mill owner's notice to the town newspaper says, "A few agitators have misled our loyal hands, who are eager to return."

- **Position and interest:** The owner wants the public to see the strike as small and illegitimate, and he wants to protect his reputation.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the mill gates and his payroll. He couldn't see what workers said to each other at home or in meetings.
- **Word choices:** "Agitators" and "misled" frame the workers as followers without grievances. "Hands" reduces them to labor.
- **Can show:** How an owner wanted the strike to look, and what vocabulary authority used.
- **Can't show:** Why workers actually struck, or how many wanted to return. That would need their own words.

Notice that the last step separates what the document *shows* from what it *claims*. Claims about other people's motives are the author's inference, not evidence.
