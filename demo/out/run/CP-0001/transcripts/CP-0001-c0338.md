2. **assistant**: That's a common sticking point, and it usually means the task is a different kind of reading than you expected. You aren't summarizing the letter. You're asking what kind of evidence it is. Reader unit 3 gives the method in four moves:

1. Name the author's position and interest.
2. List what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

Here's a parallel example on a made-up source. Imagine a mill owner writes to his investors: "A few agitators misled my loyal workers into walking out."

- **Position and interest:** He owns the mill and is writing to people who control his money. He wants to look in command.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see who left and when. He couldn't see what workers said to each other at home or at meetings.
- **Word choice:** "Agitators" and "misled" instead of "organizers" and "persuaded."
- **Can show:** How an owner described a strike to investors, and which workers he bothered to name. **Cannot show:** why the workers actually walked out.

Notice that this doesn't say whether the owner is lying. It asks what the source reveals even when he isn't trying to reveal anything.
