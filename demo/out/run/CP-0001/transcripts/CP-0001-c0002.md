**The method**
1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want?
2. **What could the author see, and what was out of view?**
3. **Notice word choices** where other words were available.
4. **Say what the document can show and what it can't.** It can show the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, and the names recorded. It can't show the motives of the people described, or what happened where the author wasn't.

Your entry is that two-column list (can show / can't show) written as prose.

**Parallel example (invented, not your source)**
Imagine a landlord's letter to the city council saying, "The tenants of Mill Row have *refused* to pay and are *agitating* the neighborhood."
- *Position and interest:* The landlord wants the council to side with him, so he presents himself as the reasonable party.
- *In view / out of view:* He sees unpaid rent but not why tenants withheld it.
- *Word choice:* "Refused" and "agitating" instead of "withheld" or "organizing."
- *Can show:* How a property owner framed a dispute for officials. *Can't show:* The tenants' reasons, or whether the neighborhood was actually disturbed.

Notice that the evaluation doesn't just say "he's biased." It says what the bias reveals and what it hides.
