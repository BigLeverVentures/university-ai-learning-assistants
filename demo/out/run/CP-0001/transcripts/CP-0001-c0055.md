**Your question:** No, "outside agitators caused the strike" isn't evidence. It's the editorial's *claim*. Treating it as evidence is the mistake Unit 2 warns about: the editorial states its conclusion in the voice of fact, so it's easy to mistake for something it has shown.

**The three pieces:**
- **Claim:** what the source asserts, in its own words.
- **Evidence:** something the source reports that you could check against other sources, such as a time, a place, or a thing someone saw.
- **Inference:** a step *you* take from the evidence. It belongs to you, not the document.

**A parallel example (not your material):** Imagine an 1890s editorial saying, "The market fire was set by the rival guild." 
- Claim: "set by the rival guild." 
- Evidence: the editorial says the fire began at the guild-hall end of the market just after the rival guild's cart was seen leaving. That is a reported detail someone could check. 
- Inference (mine): the cart's presence doesn't show anyone set a fire, and an editor with ties to one guild might be eager to blame the other.

Notice that the evidence is weaker than the claim, and the inference is about that gap.
