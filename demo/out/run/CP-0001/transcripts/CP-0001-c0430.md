**The distinction:** Your instinct is close, but "outside agitators caused the strike" is the editorial's *claim*, not its evidence. Evidence is something the source reports that a reader could check against other sources, like a date, a name, or a thing seen. The inference is the step you take from that evidence toward the claim, and it belongs to you, not the document. This is the sorting described in your Unit 2 reader.

**Practice example (not your document):** Suppose a harbor-town paper runs an editorial saying, "The dockworkers' walkout was stirred up by union men from the city."

- **Claim, in its words:** "stirred up by union men from the city."
- **Evidence, in my words:** The editorial reports that two strangers were seen talking to workers at the dock gate the morning the walkout began.
- **Inference, mine:** The strangers may have influenced the timing. But being seen talking doesn't show what they said, or whether the workers would have walked out anyway.

Notice that the evidence is a *thing reported*, and the inference is a *gap I noticed* between that report and the claim.
