**Claim, evidence, inference**
- A **claim** is what the source asserts. "The strikers were misled by outside agitators" is a claim, and your instinct to quote it as one was right.
- **Evidence** is what the source reports that you could check against other sources, like a time, a name, or something seen.
- An **inference** is your step from evidence toward a conclusion. It belongs to you, not the document.

So "outside agitators caused the strike" can't be the evidence, because it's the editorial's conclusion. If you used it as evidence, you'd be treating the author's conclusion as proof of itself, which is the skipped sorting the reading warns about.

**Practice example (not your document)**
Imagine a factory owner's newsletter saying: "The walkout was stirred up by troublemakers. Workers were seen leaving at noon, and a stranger was seen speaking at the gate."
- Claim: "stirred up by troublemakers."
- Evidence: workers left at noon, and a stranger spoke at the gate. Both are things someone could have observed.
- Inference (a reader's): the stranger may have influenced the walkout. But being seen at the gate doesn't show what was said or whether it mattered. Workers might have had their own grievances, and the owner may not have known about them.
