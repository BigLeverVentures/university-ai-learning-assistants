2. **assistant**: That's a common spot to get stuck. The worksheet isn't asking for a summary of the letter. It's asking you to treat the letter as evidence, using the method in Reader unit 3.

**The method, in four moves**
1. **Name the author's position and interest.** Who is writing, to whom, and what might they want?
2. **What could the author see, and what was out of view?** Think about where they stood.
3. **Notice word choices.** Look for places where other words were available.
4. **State what the source can show and what it cannot.** It can show the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, and the names recorded. It cannot show the motives of the people described, or events the author didn't witness.

Your 150 words are those moves written as prose, not as a list.

**A worked example on a different, invented source**

*Source: a ship captain's log entry, 1850: "The crew refused duty at dawn. The mutineers were led by the cook. I restored order by noon."*

- **Position/interest:** The captain is writing a record that owners or authorities may read, so he has reason to look in command.
- **Seen vs. out of view:** He saw the refusal and his own response. He didn't see the crew's private conversations or their reasons.
- **Word choice:** "Mutineers" instead of "sailors who refused," and "restored order" instead of "ended the dispute."
- **Can show / cannot show:** It can show how a captain framed a conflict and who he named as responsible. It cannot show why the crew refused, or whether the cook was really a leader.

Notice that I never asked whether the captain was lying. The question is what the log reveals from where he stood.
