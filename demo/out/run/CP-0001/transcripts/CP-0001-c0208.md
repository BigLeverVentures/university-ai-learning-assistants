The audience shapes what a writer includes, leaves out, and how he words things. Here's a parallel example that has nothing to do with your letter. Imagine a restaurant manager writes to the owner after a night when the kitchen staff walked out. The manager will probably stress that he kept things running, blame a few troublemakers, and say most of the staff were loyal. Would the same manager tell it that way to a friend over dinner? Probably not. The report tells you what the manager wanted the owner to believe, and that is evidence too, though of a different kind than "what happened."

That gives you two ways to read a source:
- **With the grain:** take it on its own terms. What does it report, and what does it want its reader to think or do?
