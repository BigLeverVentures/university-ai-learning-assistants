6. **assistant**: Yes, it matters, though not because audience tells you who is lying. It tells you what each writer needed the reader to believe, and that shapes what gets included, stressed, or left out.

Here's a parallel example from outside your packet. Imagine a mill manager writes to the company's owners during a walkout: "Production has stopped, but I believe most hands did not want this." Years later, a worker writes a memoir answering a company history. Both are describing the same walkout, but:

- The manager is writing *during* events, to someone who controls his job and can give him orders. He has reason to look competent and to place blame on a few troublemakers rather than on the workforce as a whole.
- The worker is writing decades later, to a public audience, in reply to an account he thinks is wrong. He has reason to defend his side's conduct and to shape what is remembered.

Neither audience makes the writer's account worthless. Each makes certain claims more likely to be emphasized, so you should check those claims especially carefully.
