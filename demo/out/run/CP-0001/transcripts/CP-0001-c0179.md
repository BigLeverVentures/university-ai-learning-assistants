**The method in four moves**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. Ask what they could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show and what it cannot.

**Parallel example (not your source):** A harbor master writes to the port board after a dockers' walkout: *"Order was kept on my wharf. The loafers who gathered at the gate were led by a man named Teague, who is no docker. I believe most hands wished to work."*

- **Position and interest:** He's a manager reporting upward, so he wants the board to see him as in control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He could see the wharf and the gate. He couldn't see what was said at the gate, or what the dockers thought privately, so "most hands wished to work" is a claim, not an observation.
- **Word choice:** "Loafers" instead of "men." "Led by" instead of "joined."
- **Can show:** How a manager framed a walkout, what he wanted his bosses to believe, and which names he recorded.
- **Cannot show:** The dockers' motives, or what happened away from his wharf.
