2. **assistant**: That's a reasonable reaction, and the Reader's unit 5 speaks to it. A ledger isn't a story, but it can show patterns once you read it in two directions:

- **Down the page:** who appears, how often, and for what.
- **Across time:** where entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening then.

Here's a parallel example on made-up material, not your page. Imagine a school's detention book for one month:

- *Down the page:* the same three students appear again and again, mostly for "talking," while "uniform" appears once. That is evidence about which rules were enforced and who got caught.
- *Across time:* there are no entries for one week. That could mean a holiday, a teacher's absence, or a lapse in enforcement. The book alone can't say which, so that part is **inference**, and you'd need other evidence to test it.
- *Silences:* the book can't tell you who talked without being caught, or what the students thought of the punishment.

So the list "shows" something because of what the clerk was required to record, and it stays silent about everything else. Keeping those three things apart (what the record was for, what it shows, and what it can't show) is the method your worksheet asks you to use.
