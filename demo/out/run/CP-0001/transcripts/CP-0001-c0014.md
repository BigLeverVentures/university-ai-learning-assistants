**The steps**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who is writing, to whom, and what they want.
2. Work out what they could see from where they stood, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the language of authority, whom they bothered to name) and what it can't (other people's motives, events the author didn't witness).

**Worked example (invented, not your source)**
Suppose a mill manager writes to the owner: *"The weavers walked out Tuesday. Most were misled by agitators. Only the Kowal brothers refused to leave their looms."*

- **Position and interest:** He's a manager reporting to his boss, probably wanting to look in control.
- **Could see / couldn't see:** He saw the mill floor. He probably didn't see what was said at the weavers' meetings.
- **Word choice:** "Misled" and "agitators" instead of "persuaded" and "organizers" frame the workers as passive.
- **Can show:** how management talked about strikers, and who counted as loyal. **Can't show:** why the weavers actually left.

In prose, that becomes a short two-column evaluation, which is what your 150 words should be.
