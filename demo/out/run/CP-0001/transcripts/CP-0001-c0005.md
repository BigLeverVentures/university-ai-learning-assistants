**The method**
1. **Author's position and interest:** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want the reader to believe or do?
2. **What they could see vs. what was out of view:** What was the author in a position to know firsthand? What happened elsewhere or inside other people's heads?
3. **Word choices:** Where did the author pick one word when others were available (the Reader's example is "ringleaders" vs. "spokesmen")?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What the document reveals (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, whose names got recorded) vs. what it can't (others' motives, events the author didn't witness).

Your 150 words are essentially that two-column list written as prose.

**Worked example (invented, not your source)**
Imagine a principal's note to the school board after students walked out over a cafeteria menu change: *"A small group of troublemakers led the rest out at lunch. I believe most students were simply following. The building was secured."*

- *Position and interest:* The principal is reporting to their bosses and wants to look in control.
- *Could see / couldn't:* They saw the hallway and the exits. They couldn't see why students chose to go.
- *Word choice:* "Troublemakers" frames the students as a problem, not as people with a grievance.
- *Can show:* How an administrator described the event, and who he thought was worth naming. *Cannot show:* what the students wanted or whether "most were just following" is true.

Notice that none of those points is a summary of the event. Each one is about the *document* and its author.
