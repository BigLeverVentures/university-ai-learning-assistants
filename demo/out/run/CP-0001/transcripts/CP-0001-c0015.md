**The method from Reader unit 3, in steps:**
1. Name the author's position and interest. Who is writing, to whom, and what do they need?
2. List what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available (the Reader's example is "ringleaders" vs. "spokesmen").
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it cannot (the motives of the people described, events where the author wasn't present).

**A worked example on a different, invented source.** Imagine a school principal's report to the board after students walked out over a new lunch policy.

- *Position and interest:* The principal is answerable to the board and wants to look in control.
- *Seen / not seen:* She saw the crowd in the yard, but not the students' conversations beforehand.
- *Word choice:* She writes "disruption" and "instigators," not "protest" and "organizers."
- *Can show:* How an administrator described student action to her superiors, and which students she knew by name. *Cannot show:* why students actually walked out, or how many agreed with the leaders.

Written as prose, that becomes a short paragraph moving from perspective to "can show" to "cannot show."
