**Short answer to your question:** no. "Outside agitators caused the strike" is the editorial's *claim*, so it can't also be the evidence for it. That's the step to fix.

**The distinction (Reader, unit 2):**
- **Claim:** what the source asserts, in its own voice, even when it sounds like plain fact.
- **Evidence:** what the source shows or reports that you could check against other sources, like a date, a name, or something seen.
- **Inference:** the step *you* take from evidence toward a conclusion. It belongs to you, not the document.

**A parallel example (not your passage):** Suppose a paper says, "The mill fire was plainly set by a rival firm."
- Claim: "set by a rival firm."
- Evidence: "a man was seen leaving the yard just before the alarm," which is something observed and checkable.
- Inference (mine): the man may have been connected to the fire, though the paper doesn't show who he was.

Notice the evidence is a *detail*, and the claim is what the paper concludes from it.
