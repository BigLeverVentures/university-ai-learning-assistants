6. **assistant**: Yes, it matters, though not in the way "who's right?" suggests. Audience doesn't tell you whether a claim is true. It tells you what pressures shaped how it was told: what the author included, stressed, or left out.

**A parallel example (not from your packet):** A factory owner writes to his board about a strike at his plant, and a worker writes years later in a memoir about the same strike. The owner is answering to people who control his job, so he may stress that the plant is safe and that most workers were loyal. The worker is writing for readers who may only have heard the owner's side, so he may stress that the workers were organized and responsible. Both could be sincere, and both could still be shaped by who they were talking to. Neither audience makes the account false, but each gives you a reason to ask what the author needed the reader to believe.

Audience is only one of the things to weigh. The other is what each person could see. The owner wasn't on the picket line, and the worker may not have seen the boardroom.
