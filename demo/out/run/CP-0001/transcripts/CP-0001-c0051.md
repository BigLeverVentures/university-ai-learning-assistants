**Three kinds of statement (Reader, unit 2)**
- **Claim:** what the source asserts about the world. Your quote, "misled by outside agitators," is a claim. The editorial states it as plain fact, but stating something confidently doesn't make it evidence.
- **Evidence:** what the source reports that you could check against other sources, like a date, a name, or something seen.
- **Inference:** the step *you* take from evidence toward a claim. It belongs to you, not the document.

So your instinct that "outside agitators caused the strike" is a conclusion. It's the editorial's conclusion, and it can't also be the evidence for itself. Treating it as evidence is the slip the unit warns about.

**A parallel example (not from your packet)**
Suppose an invented editorial says: "The mill fire was plainly set by a rival firm. Flames were seen at the loading door minutes after a strange cart left the yard."
- Claim, in its words: "set by a rival firm."
- Evidence, in my words: flames were seen at the loading door soon after an unfamiliar cart left.
- Inference, mine: the cart's driver may have started the fire. The editorial never says who the driver was or whom he worked for.
