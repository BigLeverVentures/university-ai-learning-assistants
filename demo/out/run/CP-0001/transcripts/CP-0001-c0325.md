**Practice example (invented): Harlow Mill gate book, one week in 1880**

> Mon: Pell, late, 10c. Tues: Pell, late, 10c. Tues: Voss, sleeping at loom, 25c. Wed: Pell, late, 10c. Thurs: [no entries]. Fri: [no entries; margin: "mill stopped, boiler"]. Sat: Voss, late, 10c.

**1. What was it for?** (Provenance first.) A gate clerk kept it so the mill could collect fines and track attendance. It was written for the mill's managers, not for the workers or for posterity.

**2. What it shows about discipline.**
- *Evidence:* the mill fined lateness (10c) and sleeping at the loom (25c). Pell appears three times, all for lateness.
- *Inference:* the mill treated sleeping as more serious than lateness, since the fine is higher. This is a reasonable inference, but the book doesn't state it.

**3. What it's silent about.**
- Thursday and Friday have no entries. The margin note says the mill stopped. That's a fact to explain: nobody was fined because nobody was working, not because everyone behaved.
- It doesn't show who was late and not caught, who was let off, or why Pell was late. *Inference only:* Pell might be careless, might live far away, or might be singled out. The book can't tell us which.

**4. Keeping the three apart:** purpose (attendance and fines), what it shows (which rules were enforced, and on whom), and what it can't show (motives, unrecorded breaches, workers' views).
