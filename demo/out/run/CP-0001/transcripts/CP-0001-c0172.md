**The steps**
1. **Author's position and interest:** Who wrote it, to whom, and what did they want?
2. **What they could see vs. what was out of view:** What was the author in a position to know firsthand? What happened elsewhere or inside other people's heads?
3. **Word choices:** Where did the author pick one word when another was available?
4. **Can show / cannot show:** What does the document reveal (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded)? What can it not tell you (others' motives, events the author didn't witness)?

**Worked example (not your source):** Imagine a ship's captain writes to the owners: *"The crew refused duty on Tuesday. The mutineers were led by the bosun, who has always been a troublemaker. I believe most of the men were misled."*

- **Position and interest:** The captain is writing to his employers and is responsible for the voyage. He wants to look in control and not at fault.
- **Could see vs. out of view:** He saw the refusal on deck. He couldn't see conversations in the forecastle or what the men actually wanted.
- **Word choice:** He wrote "mutineers," not "crew members who refused an order," and "misled," which assumes the men had no reasons of their own.
- **Can show:** How a captain framed a dispute for owners, and who he named as responsible.
- **Cannot show:** Why the crew refused, or whether the bosun was really the leader.

In prose, that becomes a few sentences, each naming a point and tying it to a specific phrase in the source.
