**The Reader's sentence:** "A foreman's report written to keep his position tells you about the strike, but it also tells you what a foreman thought his superintendent wanted to hear, which workers he knew by name, and which he did not."

1. **"A foreman's report written to keep his position."** This is the author's position and interest. Someone whose job is at stake writes differently than someone with nothing to lose.
2. **"Tells you about the strike."** This is reading with the grain. You take the report at face value for what it says happened.
3. **"What he thought his superintendent wanted to hear."** This is reading against the grain. The report shows the writer's assumptions, even though he didn't mean to reveal them.
4. **"Which workers he knew by name, and which he did not."** This is a small detail that reveals something. Who gets named, and who doesn't, tells you what the writer could see.

So the method is to ask what the document says, then what it reveals without intending to, and then to keep the two apart.
