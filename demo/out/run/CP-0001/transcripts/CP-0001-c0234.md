6. **assistant**: Yes, it matters, and here is a quick example from outside your packet.

Imagine a mill owner writing to his board of directors the day after a walkout. Now imagine a worker, decades later, writing for neighbors or a local historian. He might stress that the workers were organized and responsible. Both could be honest, and both could still describe the same event differently, because:

- **Audience shapes what gets included.** Each writer picks details that suit the reader in front of them.
- **Purpose shapes the wording.** A word like "abandoned" or "held" can be a description, but it can also be an argument.
- **Position limits what each could see.** One man may have been present for some things and not others, and both may be passing along what they were told.
