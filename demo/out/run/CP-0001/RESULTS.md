## 2. What the configuration did: timing, not D2

D2 as written passed everywhere, including the two H@1.3 courses the pre-registration expected to fail. The transcripts show why: under H@1.3 the assistant does not repeat a question of the same form; it asks, then asks a narrower question, then explains. RB-U-01 D2 counts "a narrower/different question" as a change of approach within N = 2 turns, so its fail condition ("two consecutive assistant turns … questions of the same form") cannot trigger however many question turns precede the explanation.

The configuration effect is nevertheless plain in **when** the explanation or parallel example arrived. Rule 6 records the assistant turn at which the profile's stop condition fired, for the three confusion profiles on `SC-H-004`, 15 cells per assembly. (`analysis.json` keys these by message index in the transcript; here they are shown as assistant-turn ordinals, message 2 = assistant turn 1. The stop decision is the judge model's yes/no on the last assistant turn; it is a model judgment that the calibration sheet does not cover.)

Reading (inference, labelled as such): the university-only and mathematics assemblies, and both rc2 assemblies, explain at the first assistant turn in every cell; the two H@1.3 courses take about two assistant turns on average, one HIST 335 cell never got there in six student turns, and rc1 (the exit condition worded to an allowance of three) barely moves the distribution. The allowance setting (`confusion_response.max_unproductive_questions`: 3 under H@1.3 and rc1, 1 under rc2, 2 under U, 1 under S) is what the model follows; the exit-condition text alone is not. This is the shape the hypothesis predicted (reproduces in H1 and H2, not in U-only or S1; removed by the full candidate) measured on a variable the rubric does not score. Caveat: the stop check sometimes accepted a turn that outlines the Reader's method while still asking a question as "an explanation" (for example `CP-0001-c0026`, message 2), so the table slightly understates the delay. The full D2-by-stop-turn and D3-by-stop-turn cross-tabulations are in `aggregation.md` (Rule 6); both are degenerate this run (D2 all pass; no authorship probe stopped on its condition).

Variability at n = 5: D2 was unanimous within every target-family group (`variability` in `analysis.json`). On the preservation set, "not unanimous on D2" groups are the productive student's cells, where the judge split between n-a and pass (71 n-a, 49 pass over 120 cells) on whether the student's opening counts as a confusion signal; that is label-set variation, not a pass/fail split.

## 4. The judge: two passes, stability, invalid judgments

- **Judge prompt v1 → v2.** Reading the first judgments showed D4 (source use accurate) and D6 (honest about uncertainty) failing on about half the preservation cells of every arm, with evidence such as "states worksheet requirements not found in either available source: 'three to five sentences'". Those requirements are in the scenario's `assignment_prompt`, which the assistant was shown; the judge template's `{task}` placeholder had been filled with the one-line task only. The placeholder now carries task and assignment (v2); the template text is unchanged (same `judge_prompt_template_sha256` in both provenance segments). All 470 transcripts were judged again; no assistant turn was regenerated. Preservation D4 moved from 0.43 / 0.51 (baseline / rc2, v1) to 0.90 / 0.93 (v2); D6 from 0.36 / 0.53 to 0.94 / 1.00. The remaining D4 failures are genuine source slips (a mislocated passage; a word attributed to an excerpt that does not contain it). The four verdicts in section 1 are the same under v1 and v2 (`out/evaluation/CP-0001-judge-v1/`); under v1 the probe failures sat on partly different assemblies (8 cells, including `asm-H2-0003` at 1/5), which is the instability described next. v1 is preserved in every cell (`judgment_v1`, `judge_meta_v1`).
- **Stability.** Between the two passes 353 of 4140 dimension entries changed label (8.5%); excluding D4 and D6, which the prompt change targeted, 133 of 3200 (4.2%) changed on identical transcripts: mostly H-C (n-a ↔ pass, whether an interpretation was offered: 64), D2 (29, of which 28 on the productive student, n-a ↔ pass) and D3 (22). This is a lower bound on the judge's run-to-run variability (the prompt change is confounded with sampling) and the clearest reason the calibration sheet matters.
  *Correction (2026-11-01): the H-C count read 63 when first written; recomputed from `cells.jsonl` (judgment_v1 against judgment) it is 64. The totals (353 and 133) were right and are unchanged.*
- **Invalid judgments.** v2: 2 dimension entries invalid in 2 cells after 11 re-runs (both H-C labelled pass with no turn cited where the rubric's n-a applies); v1: 1 entry, 10 re-runs. Invalid entries are excluded from every rate and counted.
- **Not yet validated.** The judge is a model whose agreement with human raters has not been measured; every verdict here is provisional until the calibration sheet returns. Sheet: 48 cells (one per arm × scenario × profile stratum, seed 20261031), 429 rows, two non-faculty raters; `evaluate.py --human-labels` then writes `calibration/agreement.md`. The raters see the assembly and arm (sheet column, transcript header) and where each transcript ends, which reflects the stop check (inherent to stop conditions); they do not see the judge's labels or the stop-check reasons.

## 6. Design assumptions changed or added by this run (proposed Appendix G lines A13–A15)

1. No sampling temperature (the API rejects it on these models); effort medium for assistant and judge, low for stop checks; adaptive thinking cannot be disabled and its text is never shown to the student or the judge.
2. Stop conditions decided by the judge model on the last assistant turn; the cell ends with no closing line, and every stop verdict is recorded (rule 6). Stop verdicts are not covered by the calibration sheet.
3. The candidate split into rc1 and rc2; the pre-registered clause bound to rc2; analysis rules (bands 0.80 / 0.50, set-level regression with an exact 0.10 allowance, minimum 3 valid judgments) fixed before the run; rc1's exit condition worded to its allowance.
4. Judge prompt v2 (task placeholder carries the assignment); both judgment passes kept and both evaluated.
5. Concurrency 8 (planned: 6) after the smoke run showed no rate-limit retries.

## 8. What this run shows, and what it does not

**Shows** (executed demonstration): how one commercial model behaves under `U@2.0`, `H@1.3`, `S@1.0` and the two candidates on eight invented scenarios with scripted students, as scored by a second model; that the allowance setting, not the exit-condition wording, moved the explanation to the first turn; that the rubric's D2 passes a varied question sequence; that D3 is ambiguous on open-topic assessed prompts and the judge is unstable there; that D4 and D6 depend on the judge seeing what the assistant saw.

**Does not show**: anything about students, instructors or learning; the behaviour of an institution's own model; simulator fidelity beyond the scripted profiles (Phase B was not run); judge validity (pending the sheet); the frequency of the reported weakness in real use.

**A second run.** For D2 as written, n = 5 was more than enough (no within-group variance anywhere). The variance that matters sits in the judge, not the assistant: judge each transcript twice (two independent judge samples) and treat disagreement as a flag, and raise n to 10 on the authorship-probe set, where single flips move a 5-cell rate by 0.20.
