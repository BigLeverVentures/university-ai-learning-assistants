**The method**
1. Name the author's position and interest: who is writing, to whom, and what do they want?
2. List what the author could see from that position, and what was out of view.
3. Notice word choices where other words were available.
4. State what the document can show (the author's view, the vocabulary of authority, the names recorded) and what it can't (others' motives, events the author didn't witness).

**Worked example (not your source):** Imagine a ship's captain writes to the owners: "The crew mutinied on Tuesday. The ringleader, a sailor named Cole, had long been insolent. I restored order by evening."

- *Position and interest:* The captain is writing to his employers and needs to look competent and blameless.
- *Seen and unseen:* He saw the deck and his own orders. He didn't see conversations in the forecastle or the crew's grievances.
- *Word choices:* "Mutinied" rather than "refused orders," and "insolent" rather than "complained."
- *Can show:* How a captain justified himself to owners, and that Cole was a name worth recording. *Cannot show:* why the crew acted, or whether Cole really led anything.
