**What the worksheet wants**

Reader unit 3 says evaluating a source is a two-column list written as prose:

1. **Perspective:** Who made this, and what did he want from his reader? (His position and interest.)
2. **What it can show:** The author's view, the vocabulary of authority, and the names he recorded. It can also show things he didn't mean to reveal, like what he thought his boss wanted to hear.
3. **What it cannot show:** The motives of the people he describes, and anything that happened where he wasn't.

Unit 3 also suggests looking at word choices where other words were available.

**A parallel example (not your source)**

Imagine a school principal's report to the district board after a student walkout. It might say "agitators led the students out." That tells us:
- *Can show:* how the principal described the event to his bosses, and which students he knew well enough to name.
- *Cannot show:* why the students actually left, since he's guessing at their motives.
- *Word choice:* "agitators" instead of "organizers" suggests he saw the walkout as a disruption to be blamed on someone.
