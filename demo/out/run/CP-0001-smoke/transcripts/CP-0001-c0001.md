**Parallel example (not your assignment):** A harbor master writes to the town council: *"The fishermen refused to unload on Tuesday. Two troublemakers stirred up the rest, who I believe were reluctant. I have refused to discuss prices with them."*

The method works in four moves:

1. **Author's position and interest.** He's an official reporting upward, and he wants the council to see that he handled things properly.
2. **What he could see and what was out of view.** He could see the dock and who stood where. He couldn't see the fishermen's private conversations or their reasons.
3. **Word choices.** "Troublemakers" rather than "representatives" signals his side. "I believe were reluctant" is a claim about minds he couldn't read.
4. **Can show / cannot show.**
   - *Can show:* how an official described a dispute, what he thought the council wanted to hear, and what he recorded (two individuals were singled out).
   - *Cannot show:* what the fishermen actually wanted, or whether most of them were reluctant.

The final entry is those two columns written as connected prose, not a list.
