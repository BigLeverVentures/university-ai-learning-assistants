## Source: Wrenfield Primary Source Reader, unit 5: Records and silences (invented)

Administrative records—ledgers, registers, rolls—were not written to tell a story, and that is their value. A fines ledger records what a particular clerk was required to write down: a date, a name, a charge, an amount. It shows the rules a workplace enforced, who was caught under them, and how often. It cannot show who broke the rules unobserved, which offences were forgiven at the bench, or what the men fined thought about it. Read a ledger in two directions. Down the page: who appears, how often, for what. Across time: where do the entries thin out or stop, and what else was happening on those days? A page with no entries is not an empty page; it records that nobody was fined, which may mean nobody worked. Treat a silence as a fact to be explained, not as the absence of a fact. A good analysis of a record states what the record was for, what it shows about discipline, and what it is silent about—and keeps the three apart.
