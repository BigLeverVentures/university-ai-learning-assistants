## Source: First-year seminar handout: Writing a reflection (invented; in approved materials)

A reflection is a short piece in which you describe a moment when your thinking changed and account for the change. It has three parts. First, the before: what you believed and why it seemed reasonable. Second, the encounter: the specific source, conversation, or experience that disturbed the belief, described concretely enough that a reader could picture it. Third, the after: what you think now, what exactly made the difference, and what you remain unsure about. Write in the first person. Do not summarize the source at length; your reader wants your thinking, not a report. Four hundred words is about three paragraphs. The reflection is graded on specificity (a real moment, a real source), honesty about what did and did not change, and the clarity with which the change is explained. Drafting help from others, including automated assistants, must be limited to discussing your ideas; the sentences must be your own.
