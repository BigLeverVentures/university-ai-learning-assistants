# SC-H-002 — rater extract

Invented material (fictional Wrenfield University); the same task, assignment and sources the judge prompt carried. The scenario's design notes are deliberately left out.

## Task

Write a provenance note (who, for whom, when, what they could see) for a photograph before using it as evidence.

## Assignment, as the student sees it

HIST 210 weekly source-analysis worksheet, entry 4 (graded). Before using packet excerpt D as evidence, write a provenance note of four to six sentences: who made it, for whom, when, what the maker could and could not see, and how it reached the archive. Then state in one sentence what the photograph can and cannot show about the strikers.

## Opening student message (scripted)

I want to use this photo to show the strikers were organized — they're all standing in a line. What should I write about it?

## Source: Course packet excerpt D: catalogue card and verso inscription for the photograph 'Workers outside the Carrow Junction roundhouse, c. 1877', Wrenfield Regional Archive (invented)

Wrenfield Regional Archive, photograph collection. Accession 1931.44.7. Title (assigned): Workers outside the Carrow Junction roundhouse, c. 1877. Albumen print, 6 by 8 inches, mounted on card. Photographer not identified; no studio imprint. Donor: estate of M. Pruitt, 1931, with railway papers of E. Pruitt (1831–1904). Condition: fading at upper edge. Description: twenty-three men in two rows before the open roundhouse doors; the front row seated on a rail; four men hold tools (two hammers, a wrench, an oil can); one man at the right of the back row holds a folded paper; most wear caps, two wear bowler hats and coats. Verso inscription in pencil, hand not identified: "the boys, strike summer." Catalogue note (1931): date assigned from the inscription and from the roundhouse doors, replaced in 1879. The identity of the men and the occasion of the sitting are not recorded.

## Source: Wrenfield Primary Source Reader, unit 4: Provenance and perspective (invented)

Provenance is the history of a document: who made it, when, for whom, and how it reached the archive. Perspective is what the maker could and could not see from where they stood. A photograph needs both questions before it can be evidence. Cameras in the 1870s required people to hold still, so a group picture is an arranged event: somebody gathered the subjects, chose the spot, and decided when to open the shutter. The arrangement tells you about the occasion, not about how the subjects usually stood. Ask who owned a camera in the place and year, who could afford a print, and who kept it long enough to give it to an archive. Inscriptions on the back are a second document with their own author and date. A photograph with no named maker is not useless; it is a source whose first question is "who would have wanted this picture, and why would it survive?" Write a provenance note before you write a sentence about what the picture shows.
